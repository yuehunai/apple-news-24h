# Apple 24-Hour News Policy

## Sources

Follow only the article list navigation's same-site numeric index pages, within the existing page candidate budget, until the fully dated chronological article list reaches the window start. Sidebar dates, unknown timestamps or a truncated page cannot establish coverage; failed, repeated or unavailable continuation leaves a visible coverage warning. Keep each article card’s own first text paragraph with its title and date; never borrow sidebar, adjacent-card or recommendation text. Listing dates guide discovery, while detail publication time controls final eligibility.

## Default Timezones

Fix one current system time `T` for each run. The eligible window is `(T - hours, T]`: exclude the lower endpoint and include `T`. Convert publication times to UTC before comparison; use the detected or explicitly requested local timezone for display. Do not move the window while inspecting sources.

- MacRumors: `America/Los_Angeles`
- 9to5Mac: `America/Los_Angeles`
- AppleInsider: `America/New_York`
- The Verge: prefer page metadata; default to `America/New_York` if needed
- Apple Newsroom: prefer ISO/UTC metadata; default to `America/Los_Angeles` if needed
- Chinese sources: `Asia/Shanghai`

When a detail page provides an explicit timezone or ISO offset, always trust that over the source default.

Use publisher-owned publication metadata, a locally marked publication/byline field, or a standalone date line outside article prose. Preserve document order within those visible fields. Body event dates, explicit modification/upload roles, and structured event start/end dates are not publication evidence, including when they use a `time` element or a complete date format. Related cards and inactive markup examples cannot supply the article's publication time. When detail publication evidence is absent, retain the existing dated-feed fallback and its confidence; without either, leave publication unknown.

Apple Newsroom special rule: use the article-level `NewsArticle.datePublished` or visible Newsroom publication date as the authority. Do not use `dateModified`, `article:modified_time`, page `lastmod`, RSS update time, or `VideoObject.uploadDate` to include a page in the current window, because Newsroom often refreshes those fields for media or page updates on older stories. Date-only Newsroom publication values are interpreted conservatively at midday in the Newsroom default timezone and should be labeled as date-only/low precision in diagnostics when needed.

## Candidate Discovery

Supplement broad Apple terms with unambiguous canonical branded-product and localized first-party service aliases for discovery; generic product types still require Apple context. A title-only first-party product report must not depend on feed boilerplate containing the company name. Preserve ambiguous-name context guards and the existing action, ownership and exclusion checks; recognition alone does not establish eligibility.

Merge repeated page links by normalized URL before applying relevance priority and the page budget. Prefer the complete article title and preserve listing metadata; an earlier inline fragment or comment link must not suppress a later headline for the same page.

A sparse headline describing an Apple device running or supporting third-party apps deserves detail inspection even after its RSS excerpt rolls away. The device owns that hosting capability; a third-party app release or a competitor's Apple comparison does not receive this recall exception. Detail eligibility remains separate from final relevance.

Vendor-neutral shipment or market-share headlines may proceed to detail verification when they identify an Apple-relevant device market and a report period. Detail admission and inclusion use locally attributed Apple results, including those after the lead or in a table; an Apple mention elsewhere is insufficient. Component-price context does not override a verified current Apple measurement. Keep each result-bearing source passage in selected facts or required review when the display shortlist omits it. Joint vendor totals, competitor values, historical comparisons and forecasts do not establish Apple's individual result for the current period. Keep observation qualifiers before the vendor name as well as after it; a denied or forecast value must not become an observed statistic through grouping keys. An observed rank follows the same rule as a numeric result. In a focused report with an observed joint result, a bounded joint ranking may apply to each named member (for example, two brands occupying the top two); it does not establish their exact order or assign their combined share to either brand. Preserve forecasts in independently eligible reports as forecast facts and group them separately from observed results. Keep table cells, headers, units and current/comparison periods distinct. Resolve an explicitly relative current year from the source publication date. A financial revenue figure or a historical low must not supply the period of a shipment measurement.

For an eligible market measurement report, preserve a quantitative headline claim that is not represented in its retained source facts, with the publisher's own scope and attribution. Equal numbers in a different metric, period, direction or subject do not prove coverage. A headline without a captured text unit has source-text-only provenance; do not invent body offsets or borrow the body report's named institution as its speaker. This does not promote speculative headlines or make adjacent editorial wording a required fact.

When a detail fetch falls back to the discovery excerpt, the ordinary JSON includes a required source review with `capture_status: discovery-only` and its available `fetch_error`, even without diagnostic output. Processing every captured sentence does not establish full-source coverage. This marker changes neither publication time nor relevance; recover material missing evidence from the original page when available, otherwise preserve the limitation in review notes. Apply the same action rules to recovered passages: revise affected event or fragment dispositions and assign each material passage to its supported action. An additional bullet requires a distinct eligible action; the excerpt-only boundary does not establish one.

Paragraph-link excerpts remain summary evidence and cannot replace an available page or feed headline merely by being longer; preserve this distinction across source entry points.

Admission follows the headline-owned subject and action. A product-name path such as Apple Podcasts or a compatible-device list mentioning another vendor must not veto a direct Apple report. Preserve genuine retailer discount and publisher-episode exclusions. A documented institutional purchase is procurement, not a retail deal merely because it names an amount. Detail verification and source-owned relevance still decide publication.

Discard executable script and style blocks before selecting HTML body containers or paragraphs; markup embedded in code is not article evidence. Publication metadata is still read separately from the original page.

Remove explicit serialized product cards and their resource URLs before splitting structured article bodies into sentences. Keep adjacent factual prose and independently reported prices; a shopping-card label or tracking address is not a required news fact.

A newsletter attribution in article prose is reporting, not a subscription widget. Preserve a current disclosure following an older background paragraph; new event-format information can qualify without a changed date or product list. Signup prompts remain noise.

A title reporting a future hardware configuration keeps that product-roadmap action even when supporting text discusses operating-system compatibility. A title about an OS support change stays a software action; device-testing background must not give it the hardware roadmap identity. A shared component key cannot promote a demonstrated competitor comparison or an editorial recap with no new reporting. Keep a source’s explicit denial of that configuration separate from the affirmative report; an unrelated denial in the background does not change the primary action.


Use RSS/Atom first. If a required source fails or looks sparse:

1. Try the source homepage, Apple/channel pages, and date archive pages for both the current local date and previous local date.
2. Search the site for Apple product and system terms: Apple, iPhone, iPad, Mac, macOS, iOS, watchOS, visionOS, AirPods, Apple Watch, Apple Intelligence, Siri, App Store, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV, CarPlay.
3. Use web search with `site:<domain>` and the current/previous local date.
4. For Chinese daily digests such as morning briefings, extract each distinct Apple-specific subitem as its own candidate. Exception: skip IT之家 `IT早报` pages, 爱范儿 `早报` pages, and MacRumors weekly `Top Stories:` pages at candidate-discovery time, because they are link roundups whose Apple items should be covered by their linked standalone articles. Match these recurring columns only for their own source and established title prefix so an unrelated publisher, app, or article using similar words is not excluded. Keep non-Apple subitems out even when the roundup headline, metadata, or nearby paragraph mentions Apple as unrelated background.

Across all configured sources, homepages, channel/index pages, feeds and discovery endpoints are navigation surfaces, not article candidates; apply this boundary before spending detail or page-link budgets. Apple Newsroom discovery admits dated Newsroom article paths, including country-localized paths. Account, store, app-download, archive and other navigation links are not article candidates even when their labels mention Apple. Apply this boundary before the HTML link budget and to feed candidates.

On crowded listing pages, apply discovery eligibility before spending the page-link budget. Scan the captured page for eligible reports beyond unrelated links, preserve their page order, and keep the existing cap and detail-fetch budget. Passing discovery does not establish publication time or final relevance.

A personal purchase and receipt-inspection walkthrough is a product review, not a new Apple action. Retail prices, inspection duration and compatibility checks alone do not establish a hardware measurement report; independently supported quantitative tests still follow the measurement rules.

When a source feed is complete but still misses individual current-window posts from a high-volume source, use a structured first-party endpoint such as a WordPress posts API before broad web search, with enough depth to cover late-day posts rather than only the first short page of results. Treat API/listing timestamps as discovery hints only; the detail page's precise published time remains authoritative. A dedicated report of current first-party functionality, support coverage or operating conditions may be a candidate when its reliable detail-page publication time is inside the window. The capability may have debuted earlier; preserve its current status without inventing a new rollout. Evergreen instruction pages, buying guides, generic guide/tag pages, and other stories that merely use an established capability as background remain excluded.

When feeds or pages expose source-side category hints, keep those hints as candidate context. Category labels such as Apple TV, Apple Music, App Store, iMessage, Apple Wallet, or equivalent Chinese terms may establish Apple service context for a story whose title is otherwise too short or brand-light. Use that context for relevance, event kind, and detail-page prioritization, but do not print raw category labels as standalone facts.

After a detail page is fetched successfully, its title and first substantive body text control article identity. Feed/list context may enrich the same subject, but it must not upgrade a non-Apple detail-page subject into an Apple event or attach an unrelated Apple action from a nearby/related item. Keep discovery context only when it is compatible with the detail-page subject and action; a failed detail fetch may still use the documented low-confidence fallback below.

Keep admission separate from final relevance: a title-led direct Apple subject with a reported action or substantive lead must reach detail verification even when its verb is absent from an old action-word list or its event kind is general company news. Apply the same rule to the cleaned detail article, so removing feed boilerplate cannot make a genuine story disappear. Bare product-name navigation is not a report, and admission alone never promotes a third-party, opinion, or roundup item into the brief. Judge a newly published first-party communication by that publication action rather than by the date of an older incident it describes; a historical anecdote with no new Apple communication remains subject to the normal exclusion rules. A current Apple executive’s new memorial post qualifies on that basis; an old obituary or a former executive’s personal reminiscence alone does not. Keep different honorees and anniversary periods distinct.

Treat the normalized title-led event identity as authoritative across relevance, classification, and reconciliation. Decide content form and action ownership from the source itself: a direct first-party Apple subject plus a current concrete action may remain strong across languages, while buying advice, deals, tutorials, podcasts, opinion/analysis, multi-item roundups, third-party spotlights, competitor comparisons, and Apple-platform-only compatibility remain weak unless their title and opening lead establish a separate qualifying Apple action. Publisher-selected app recommendations remain third-party spotlights when the title and opening establish that selection; later device specifications do not promote the whole article. Independently reported first-party functionality in the same source still requires a separate fragment disposition. Ignore an extracted lead sentence that merely repeats the normalized title so the first substantive sentence can supply a missing action or subject; later body metrics, related links, analyst names, product names, or background Apple references must never override that decision. When the crawler deliberately projects a genuine multi-product hardware report into product-scoped child events, require the title or opening lead to establish that multi-product scope, make each generated child title and product-specific fact set its authoritative boundary, and keep unrelated historical or compatibility examples from becoming synthetic child events. A named first-party physical product disclosed through OS code remains a hardware event because the system build is the evidence location, while an unscoped system-resource leak may remain a software/system disclosure.

A source-owned announcement of a new named Apple retail campaign on Apple's own store is a first-party action; preserve channel, timing and conditional benefits rather than treating the combined price as unconditional. Keep a retailer's currency-off/low-price promotion, a writer's personal complaint and an explicitly retrospective weekly column weak; background features, legal-sounding words or recalled rollouts cannot promote them. A retailer selling a separately paid purchase entitlement owns that transaction; the referenced Apple device does not make the fee its device price or an Apple pre-order action. Keep independent source-local product or supply reports available for evidence review without promoting the retail transaction. Weekly Apple-device sales reports bind the product generation, market, reporting week, observed/forecast stage and quantity; a later comparison must not replace the first owned measurement.

Bind retail reports to the device and concrete operation: planned storefront maintenance, current closure, scheduled preorder opening, opened orders, delivery observations and an official demand response are distinct. Delivery observations retain their local estimated date, market and configuration scope; publication dates do not identify the observation. A current tracked average waiting duration is also a delivery observation: preserve the observer, channel and measured duration, independently of supporting order limits or resale prices. Identical amounts do not identify a survey; copied complete observations may reconcile, while independently worded reports without a shared observation anchor remain for review. Merge copied observations while keeping conflicting estimates separate. Resale quotes alone are not completed transactions; preserve reported transactions and their before/after-opening stage. A forecast, purchase guide or retailer deal does not acquire a preorder action from background. Historical, denied or other-model statements cannot establish the current operation.

Internal store instructions about walk-in purchases, an attributed reporter's stock forecast, and Apple's demand statement retain separate actions even when all describe scarce supply. Translations of the same instruction or measurement should reconcile; normalize device-name spacing while retaining the measurement's week, region, cumulative/weekly scope, quantity and observed/forecast stage. Carrier pricing belongs to the named provider and currency in its own clause, including plan-specific free access; another carrier's background price cannot supply it. Apple's own accessory construction remains first-party hardware even when reported as an installation caveat. Independent app adaptation and a vendor's accessory-tool withdrawal remain third-party actions. Personal deployment instructions do not become a new OS announcement. In a commentary article, separately bound first-party operating requirements can be reported while the surrounding evaluation remains in review. A current executive conference appearance owns its event and hardware/company category, not a prior leadership transition mentioned in the biography.


Treat editorial calls for Apple to add, adopt, or imitate a feature as opinion/analysis when the title and opening lead propose a future change rather than report an attributed current action; a body recap of real existing OS features must not promote that proposal. Determine action ownership before platform relevance across languages: when a non-Apple vendor announces, tests, previews, or rolls out its own app, accessory, service, or branded experience for an iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Vision Pro, or another Apple platform, the vendor remains the subject and the article stays third-party context unless Apple separately changes the product, platform, policy, approval, or interoperability behavior.

When a headline supplies a complete subject and concrete predicate, preserve that identity before reading background for additional merge keys. Bind statistical region and period as explicit qualifiers: missing scope is not a conflicting scope, but a source with missing scope cannot bridge two explicitly different measurements. A company name modifying a supplier or competitor does not identify the actor; similarly, an Apple device used as material for an independent replica does not make that project an Apple publication. This does not exclude a newly reported benchmark of the Apple-device setup itself: distinguish measured results and their configuration from theoretical targets, comparison hardware and a display-only project. Normalize project names separately from release verbs and bind operating-system compatibility milestones to the project, target chip generation, and development/release stage; component-driver milestones remain distinct and platform enablement belongs to software/ecosystem news, not a new Mac hardware release. Bind event participation to the named participant, event, and positive/negative participation state; a background event date or leadership succession must not turn an absence report into a schedule or appointment story. A reported review of a named Apple service's monetization model is distinct from an enacted price change or a feature update, even when it discusses possible fees. Headlines recommending that readers perform an activity with an app are app-use guidance rather than a new Apple action, including when only a feed excerpt is available; mentions of Apple events or devices do not establish first-party ownership. These rules do not reduce discovery or detail-page coverage, and weak items remain traceable in the review queue.

For kernel support reports, preserve the component (such as storage versus graphics), target chip generation, and submitted/merged stage; an existing downstream OS release cannot supply the status of an upstream patch. Official technical drawings and published device model-capacity figures are eligible when Apple owns the affirmative publication in the opening assertion, even if the headline uses a comparison or the body discusses accessories. Bind the published object in that same assertion; unrelated Apple updates, third-party estimates, negations, and historical publications do not qualify. A personal purchase or upgrade recommendation does not qualify merely by listing specifications or impressions. A source-owned quantitative test with a stated method or configuration and measured Apple-hardware results is independently eligible, including when an individual reviewer performs it; retain that finding and its conditions while excluding adjacent buying advice. A personal review after days, weeks, or months of use cannot project its recollection of a product launch into a new launch event. A headline explicitly recalling a release years ago, paired with an anniversary-recollection opening, likewise remains weak; a new anniversary edition, update or newly disclosed historical evidence retains its own current action. Classify a patent dispute by the technology at issue: a haptic mechanism belongs to hardware, while a software-service patent stays in software even if a device is mentioned as background. Each source must retain that category when other coverage leaves the rolling window.

Classify an accessory by the object of its current action: physical products belong to hardware, while their drivers, firmware and apps belong to software. Compatibility or bundled configuration software does not change a physical product’s category, and category alone never promotes Apple relevance.

Normalize a third-party app or accessory event by its independent owner plus the concrete action, with the Apple platform treated only as a qualifier; translated or differently ordered headlines about the same owner launching the same Mac/iPhone/iPad/Watch/TV/Vision app or Apple Home-compatible product should reconcile into one `weak` event rather than several strong Apple events unless Apple itself changes the platform, policy, approval, or interoperability. Conversely, Apple-owned brands such as Beats and explicitly Apple-made physical products remain first-party hardware subjects when retail listings or code expose a concrete model, identifier, or specification. Code-derived Home Hub/HomePad interface capabilities remain software/system changes and may reconcile across headlines that describe the same widgets, faces, or Smart Stack behavior from hardware and software perspectives. A provisional same-product cluster must still split when one report makes a concrete hardware claim and another only reports or comments on a distinct OS feature; the shared Apple product name is not same-action evidence. Routine retailer price cuts, third-party historical archives, and broad multi-product launch projections remain `weak` and must not bridge into a concrete product event.

Keep named third-party app spotlights `weak` when the reported action belongs to the independent app or developer and an Apple platform is only the runtime or audience; an OS-version prefix or supported-device list is a qualifier, not an Apple compatibility action, and must not promote the outside app during reconciliation. Editorial leadership scorecards, biographical/personality profiles without a current role or strategy action, retrospective wins-and-misses lists, and resurfaced historical interviews also remain `weak`; a current attributed report with new quantified company performance, or a current executive statement made as part of an active transition, remains eligible. An official Apple obsolete/vintage-list update with a concrete repair, parts, or service consequence is a direct hardware lifecycle action. A direct statement that Apple will not repair or service the listed devices also establishes that consequence; another repair provider’s refusal or a denial of ending service does not. A current list update can also be established by Apple assigning a named device a lifecycle status, even when a headline omits the word list. Bind the listed models and explicit status to that action, preserve differing model years, and keep historical comparison devices outside its identity. An ambiguous translated label or a conflicting source definition does not by itself establish a repair cutoff. A sufficient captured excerpt retains its news eligibility while unavailable detail remains a capture limitation. First-party app or service renames reconcile only when the product, renamed object, and new label agree; when different writing systems prevent literal label comparison, require the same first-party app, rename action, named-object class, and explicit region. When a title identifies a specific Apple executive but abbreviates the change, the bounded opening lead may supply a concrete current role-exit action for cross-source reconciliation. Classify by the title's grammatical owner: a named physical Apple product being tested or developed remains hardware even when Siri, AI, or another software capability is a qualifier, while a home-security subscription or monitoring-service roadmap remains software/service news and must not bridge into a HomePod or other Home hardware roadmap. A title-owned feature change in a named, current OS train remains software/system news even when the affected iPhone, iPad, Mac, Watch, TV, or Vision hardware is named; only a title-led physical component, prototype, production, or hardware disclosure moves it to hardware/products.

Treat a focused report about one current, versioned first-party app as that app's concrete OS-feature event even when an English headline uses `what's new`; translated coverage of the same component and changes should reconcile with it, not remain a separate broad roundup. First-person product enthusiasm and historical causal retrospectives remain `weak` when they recap existing features, leaks, or past events without independent current reporting. When a structured code or resource-list report is projected into product-level child events, require evidence local to each child such as an identifier, codename, model, prototype, test, delay, cancellation, development, or production claim; release-calendar background and generic event expectations must not create synthetic product events. Keep a disclosure explicitly framed as a set of still-unidentified or unresolved device identifiers as one aggregate event unless the source itself assigns a concrete product action to an individual identifier; nearby known-model strings are context, not a product-level child action.

A newly sourced schedule for an additional batch of Apple product announcements is one launch-campaign action. Bind its reported period and stage; the prior event date and speculative product inventory are context, not separate product disclosures or an OS release. Preserve uncertainty about the lineup and event format. Do not infer a roundup from punctuation: a colon, comma, slash, or several specifications can describe one attributed hardware report. Distinguish a focused sourced inference from an explicit collection of earlier rumors or an editorial preview without new reporting. Historical comparisons of unchanged specifications across an executive's tenure are analysis, not a new product action; a leadership announcement mentioned as background does not change the comparison's subject. Report structure and freshness are separate checks; preserve attribution and dates of underlying evidence, and do not invent a new first-disclosure time merely because an older diagram or report is being discussed again. A reporter's name, a leaked-part adjective, or a background acronym must not make a first-party product disclosure into the reporter's own third-party product action.

Treat a forecast that Apple will withdraw or discontinue multiple current products as one catalog-lifecycle event, even when the article enumerates every affected family and mentions their replacements; do not project that list into separate product roadmaps or merge it with launch timing for one incoming model. `Remove`, `pull`, `下架`, or `移除` means an App Store removal only when the title or opening lead explicitly establishes an app/application and Apple or the App Store as the removal actor; an Apple Online Store product withdrawal must remain a hardware catalog event. Separate URLs from the same publisher require positive same-event evidence such as a shared concrete action, exact event relation, or matching product change; a shared Apple product, generation, changed vocabulary, publication time, or body background alone is not enough. Conversely, a cross-platform OS beta/release train with the same version and stage remains one software release event and must not be split by hardware-oriented cleanup merely because visionOS coverage mentions Vision Pro. Routine roundups of independently maintained CarPlay or other Apple-platform apps remain `weak` when Apple introduces no platform API, policy, approval, interoperability, or first-party capability change.

Apply that authority consistently when the article body contains misleading Apple context. A non-Apple title subject must not become an Apple event because a later biography mentions Apple-platform work, a user-built utility happens to run on Apple hardware, or an aggregate security comparison includes macOS statistics without a concrete Apple flaw, attack path, patch, or policy action. An unattributed multi-product roadmap, countdown, `here's what's coming`, or `here's what we know` editorial remains a roundup rather than a new Apple action even when it repeats event timing and detailed prior rumors. Conversely, a title-led forecast of an Apple product's consumer price remains eligible even when competitor pricing appears as context; keep it separate from manufacturing/component-cost analysis, retailer discounts, buying advice, and unchanged analyst commentary. When a multi-brand or market-wide price action explicitly says an Apple product did not change price, Apple is only the unchanged comparator and the article remains `weak`; a speculative aside that Apple may change price later does not transfer ownership of the current action. For copied cross-source coverage, an otherwise identical normalized headline may be used as exact duplicate evidence after stripping publisher suffixes, but a merely similar headline, shared product, metric, date, or generic action is not sufficient.

Treat a criminal or civil case about non-Apple parties as `weak` when Apple Watch, iPhone, Health, call-log, location, or other device records are merely evidence and Apple did not take a new legal, platform, privacy, or product action. Conversely, differently framed reports about the same regulatory financial impact may reconcile when one headline emphasizes Apple's Services business and another emphasizes App Store commission pressure, but only when their opening evidence establishes the same regulatory cause and the same service/commission result; a new fee schedule, commercial terms, ruling, filing response, or enforcement action remains a separate event.

When a candidate is selected for detail-page fetching but that detail request fails, do not automatically discard the story if it is not an Apple Newsroom page and the feed or listing already provides a parseable timestamp plus useful summary/context. The crawler may keep the item as a low-confidence discovery fallback and record it in `selected_detail_fetch_failures` and `source_discovery_fallback_counts`. This is a reliability fallback for transient detail-page failures; it is not a substitute for detail-page time verification when the detail page is available.

A device-purchase crime remains weak when Apple is only the object. Same-incident body evidence that explicitly identifies a failure of Apple-operated store identity or payment verification makes it a direct service-security report, not ordinary device crime; preserve the disclosing party, unconfirmed cause, canceled-transaction status, and any Apple response. Do not borrow a historical, denied, or unrelated merchant's mechanism. Registered technical component names must also remain recognizable next to Chinese text; do not demote a current built-in-system support report merely because a Unicode word boundary hides its component.

For source-local classification, bind the actor and concrete operation before considering device names or background paragraphs. A dedicated report of a device’s provided customizable display faces is a software capability report; bind the first-party device and affirmative capability rather than promoting tutorials, wishes or third-party themes. A comparison target does not own the competitor’s specifications. Bind display behavior and policy revisions to the acting device or organization in the same local assertion; a developer mentioning Apple rules is not Apple revising them. Treat cited podcasts as attribution when the article reports a concrete action; the publisher’s podcast recap remains editorial. An attributed quotation of someone else’s opinion does not turn an independent report into the author’s evaluation. Service outages, catalog additions, retail material deliveries, software setup discoveries, and hardware finish disclosures retain their own objects and operations. Personnel changes require a people or job predicate: a replaceable part and an executive’s job title do not establish a workforce change. A color/flavor description containing “Apple” does not identify Apple as the product owner. A third-party issuer's share-price reaction is its own market action even when an Apple verdict caused it. Publisher recommendations and subjective product evaluations retain their editorial form despite later specifications or compatibility facts. A named Apple hardware development program remains direct news when the source reports current work; competitor examples and cancelled older programs cannot replace its owner or project code.

A regional service beta plan keeps its own release stage, territory and schedule; do not borrow the version of a later public-release plan from background. Internal OS testing requires a source-local version and internal-test or log observation, not a generic testing mention elsewhere.

Software operations keep their software category when reported on Apple hardware: OS releases, built-in capabilities discovered in code, and an acknowledged upcoming software repair are distinct from physical device changes. Merge code discoveries only for the same device/platform, capability, and discovery stage; do not merge them with an actual release. Bind historical and negative qualifiers to the discovery claim itself; an earlier announced capability in the background does not make a new code discovery historical. A generic mixed-topic warning must not reopen a group when every source shares one explicit primary claim and every pair passes the unified matcher; keep other warnings and all conflict checks. A dedicated current report of a named first-party capability, support scope, cost or operating conditions remains eligible even when it reports no new rollout in the window. Its main subject must be that capability or scope; an OS version or a recalled “added” feature in another story does not qualify usage advice or background as a separate report. For current multi-version release reports, project each explicitly released platform/version from the headline or a locally introduced release inventory. Keep each build number with its source row, leave ambiguous background for review, and never infer a release from a compatibility list.

For current multi-action organization or creative-software reports, project only source-local operations established by the headline/opening and their owned body units. Keep development/management plans, service-revenue plans, actual functional-team reductions, and the disposition of a customer-support replacement plan distinct. A planned cut, a held/cancelled plan and an actual reduction are different stages; a denial must not become an affirmative stage. Company-wide organization and explicitly hardware-engineering or physical-product teams belong to hardware/products; AppleCare support and software/service teams belong to software/systems. Background product roadmaps do not create extra product events. Verify the local actor and polarity before binding a body assertion. Split independent assertions inside a paragraph; a transfer condition follows its own team, while a mixed team list without separable assertions stays in the review queue. Attach the functional category to each bound claim, so another source or the parent article cannot change it.

Software updates retain their application or firmware subject even when they enable new capture hardware. Separate a creative-suite update from a separate filming-app update, keeping prices, support conditions and unassigned source fragments traceable. Firmware identifiers belong to the current release statement and its beta/final stage; old stable-build references cannot identify the new release. Denied or historical releases do not establish a current update. Generic operation recognition must preserve existing source-local model, region, investigator, part code, and before/after qualifiers; a title's background clause cannot own the action. Device teardown, user fault reports and an Apple software-fix commitment stay separate. Reconcile duplicate production, preorder-preparation and fault reports by their named device and operation; order preparation is not the opening of orders. Weekly service-performance reports retain the reported week and cannot merge with a trailer release; the adaptation's book is not the released show's identity.

A third-party launch, hardware customization, speculative business-risk analysis or device-purchase crime does not become an Apple action from Apple background. A captured incident that concretely disrupts Apple-platform applications, or tests demonstrating unpermitted access to a first-party OS data store, may qualify as an ecosystem incident; ordinary app features, permitted data imports and broad risk predictions stay weak. Preserve the reporting actor, measured scope, permission conditions and attribution rather than presenting a third-party claim as an Apple-confirmed finding. A new Apple-owned guide publication and a concrete current API disclosure qualify by their publication/disclosure action; unofficial guides and retrospective interviews do not.

## Cache Handling

The script cache is only for inspecting the current run's successful HTTP responses. By default, the crawler uses `apple-news-24h` under Python's platform temporary directory, clears the directory at startup, and writes a marker file plus fresh responses. For automation runs, write the full JSON result with a fixed `--output` path; the script writes this file after cache cleanup and uses atomic replacement. Use `--cache-dir` when an automation needs a predictable cache location. Do not read old cache files as source material or use cache contents to decide whether an event is inside the current 24-hour window.

For final automation briefs, read the complete freshly written JSON after the run finishes. `events` and `deferred_events` are current source-supported event results; `required_final_brief_titles` is a review checklist. `final_brief_queue` also contains unresolved source fragments, so it does not simply mirror the event list. `brief_output`, `final_brief_markdown`, and an adjacent `*.brief.md` are coverage scaffolds, not a finished brief or an unconditional keep list. Resolve every eligible item using the rules below. Stdout status, older JSON, cached pages from previous runs, previews, prior summaries, and automation memory cannot define current news.

## Source Evidence and Unassigned Material

A source can report more than one action. Keep each independently supported claim tied to its local subject, predicate, qualifiers, and source facts. The first substantive opening sentence may complete a title; later historical examples, quoted background, comparisons, captions, and unrelated links do not acquire authority over the current action merely by sharing a product or person.

Source-local supporting facts use the precise sentence or clause that states a current mechanism, limit, observation, or attributed report. Keep factual parts of mixed paragraphs with their original spans; writer analogies, buying advice, unsupported predictions, unattributed motive or legal-merits judgments, image credits, and past failed proposals do not become current product conditions. A neighboring reported action or limit retains its own exact source span. A compatible accessory's pairing method may support the current device without importing a separate device's storage behavior.

A separate future or historical stage continues across neighboring sentences until the source explicitly returns to the current action. Keep an author's current test result as an attributed observation, and distinguish free download from optional subscriptions without importing adjacent navigation text.

Follow `events[].sources[].evidence_id` or the same field under `deferred_events` into the `source_evidence` registry for the original source identity, title, opening text, publication evidence, and captured units. A source-review queue entry also carries `evidence_id`. Each event's `fact_sources[].origins` records the origins of final facts. A located origin points to the source article, cleaned unit text, and character interval `[start, end)`; a source-text-only origin is source-level support, not a fabricated exact quotation location. A fact from another source in the same event does not prove that this source made the same claim.

Entries with `unprocessed_evidence` in `final_brief_queue` need a decision from their own context. They may contain an independent action, an important qualification of an existing action, or material that the policy excludes. Review them even when other parts of the source have already been projected or the source produced no parent article. A locally attributed current result remains reviewable without promoting the surrounding multi-topic report. Neither a required flag nor the parent event's tier automatically makes a fragment eligible for publication. Preserve important amounts, complete applicable lists, versions, regions, time limits, uncertainty, attribution, and official responses; do not drop them solely because display summaries are shorter than the source.

Only treat a fragment as already consumed when its own source evidence is fully represented in the selected final content. Identical words elsewhere in a page, another source's fact, an unselected candidate fact, or a fuzzy text match do not establish that coverage. Editorial bylines, subscription navigation, related links, and other demonstrated template material may be excluded with a reason while retaining the original evidence for inspection. A short list or a personal name alone is not template evidence: news appointments, compatible devices, eligibility, prices, countries, and source attribution can all be material facts.

Preserve list membership through nested paragraph or layout wrappers. Short members must reach the selected facts or required source review with their local conditions; an aggregate range does not cover differently labelled fees or measurements. Explicit prerequisite lists rendered as short paragraphs retain their members and introductory conditions, including semicolon-separated rows. Preserve source-stated purpose exclusions as review evidence when omitted from the fact shortlist. List markup alone does not establish ownership or eligibility.

For source-local support facts, an explicitly named peer product can change the owner of following implicit-subject sentences; a generic product-family mention alone does not establish a different owner. An app or service described as a feature of the titled device or OS remains in that source action's fact or review path. When a single-subject parent article's body discusses another peer product, keep that product's unassigned qualification with its exact source span in `unprocessed_evidence` instead of counting it as a selected fact of the parent event.

If the script is entirely unavailable, collect current pages using the source and fallback steps above. A Markdown record of the fixed window, source URLs, publication evidence and timezone conversion, concrete actions, inclusion/merge/exclusion decisions, and material facts is sufficient; there is no requirement to manufacture the crawler schema before drafting.

Bind reported operations before column labels and headline wording: retail personalization remains a first-party retail action; platform-owned diagnostic commands remain software functionality even when described in an enterprise column. A dedicated dated product announcement preview is not a multi-topic roundup merely because its subtitle says “what to expect”. Consumer purchase-intent surveys remain weak; product specifications in their supporting text do not turn opinions into observed sales.

Candidate admission must preserve a title-owned Apple action even without a named product. A counterparty name or an incidental exclusion word in supporting text does not veto detail verification; a figurative “giveaway” can mean evidence, not a reader contest. Actual reader contests, retailer promotions and third-party actions remain subject to their own content and ownership rules. Admission does not promote relevance or confirm a rumored action.

For coordinated order adjustments, retain the source-owned batch of model and direction pairs, with its stated period; reservation counts, component costs and supplier commentary do not define that batch. For activation measurements, a directly connected breakdown may inherit the reporting period from its introduction; unrelated intervening text, forecasts and negation break that connection. Preserve explicit region, year, period and statistical scope conflicts.

Treat an initiative’s financing, implementation and reported progress as facets of one substantive update when the evidence presents them together. A later executive post explaining its results is attribution, not another underlying action merely because its verb, metric or publication time differs. Separate initiatives, changed commitments or stages and substantive responses still require separate boundaries; inspect a referenced announcement when its relation is material and unclear. Likewise, comparison periods, model breakdowns and ranks within one reported dataset remain its supporting series; period mismatch separates independently reported measurements, not each fact in that series.

Resale reports need report-level anchors. A dealer’s order is identified by its participant, explicit batch and quantity, and locally attributed order total or deposit. A changed quote or later balance payment is progress on that order, not another batch; do not treat every payment as a deposit. A recalled order alone is background; its facts may support a locally bound current update without becoming a new event. Equal prices, release dates, or product names cannot join unrelated observations. Keep buy-side bids, sellers’ asking prices, completed sales, costs and forecasts attributed to their samples. Multiple samples organized into one market report may remain together; a distinct interview or order within the same article may support a separate event. Missing attributes do not bridge conflicting batches or configurations. A quote may continue an immediately preceding observation of the same device; retain the contextual source span and stop at a different sample, action, historical comparison or forecast. Republications may reconcile when their complete ordered observation sentences and sample conditions agree despite paragraph layout; a shared price or partial overlap is insufficient.

A current source-local capability, availability statement or observed physical construction can be retained from an otherwise editorial article. Bind the actual first-party app or accessory before extracting its facts, keep version and operating conditions, and leave verdicts, predictions and instructions outside the independent claim. A generic app name on an Apple device does not establish Apple ownership. Platform support matrices retain the stated software version and supported-device restrictions; a current matrix is distinct from its historical announcement. Device-provisioning reports bind the model, region, profile limit and activation method in the same current source passage; matching conditions may reconcile republications, while historical comparisons and other models cannot supply that identity. Regulatory specifications require the actual document publisher and publication status; an unofficial label or a denial is not an Apple publication. Keep a plan, its cancellation and an explicit response distinct when they are different substantive actions; differing descriptions of one plan do not alone require a split. A shared model name does not establish a shared action; model-generation numbers are not spelling variations. Keep announcement and on-sale dates attached to their roles when matching reports of the same product batch.

## Inclusion Rules

Keep new or materially updated news about:

- OS releases, beta/RC updates, security fixes, vulnerabilities, enterprise fixes, platform policy changes.
- A currently used web exploit against iOS is a software/security action even when a fake retail page is the lure and the exploited flaw was patched earlier. Require source-local evidence of the platform attack and delivery path; ordinary credential forms and third-party app flaws do not qualify. Bind the named exploit to that current campaign, preserve targeted-data lists and attributed patch dates, and keep genuine retail dates as context rather than a second launch action.
- A narrative headline can still report Apple's concrete physical implementation. Bind the implementing actor, component and host device; generic integration wording does not make an Apple engineering report a third-party action. Preserve attributed confirmations and operational facts from long paragraphs at their source spans, without importing author verdicts or hypothetical implementations. Recovered long support follows existing concise details in the display budget; unselected support remains in source-local review.
- HomeKit platform expansion and capability changes are software/system actions. Device launches mentioned as background do not change that subject or join it to a hardware roadmap; ordinary third-party HomeKit compatibility remains weak accessory news.
- CarPlay is an Apple platform. iOS/WWDC-era CarPlay feature, API, Siri, media-interface, AirPlay, navigation, or platform-behavior changes are software/system news even when the article discusses third-party apps; routine third-party CarPlay app availability remains `weak` unless Apple changes the platform, policy, approval, or interoperability behavior.
- Apple services: Apple Music, TV+, Arcade, iCloud, Apple One, AppleCare+, App Store, Apple Pay, Apple Card, Apple Intelligence, Siri. Apple service/content events that pass the crawler's relevance and time-window rules remain eligible service news, including Apple TV+ show season, review/status, cast/production, Apple Music chart/playlist, Apple Arcade/content, AppleCare+ coverage or payment-option changes, and Apple services offer items; do not drop them merely because they are entertainment/content coverage, single-source, niche, or lighter than major OS or hardware news. App Store, iCloud, Apple Account, Apple Pay, or Apple Card payment-method restoration, payment compliance, card-tokenisation, regional payment-option, or billing-availability changes are Apple service/business actions when Apple is the actor or platform operator.
- Apple-owned apps, brands, and Apple-operated activity programs remain first-party subjects when the title and lead report a concrete update, engineering practice, product strategy, platform action, or user challenge. This includes Shazam feature releases, Beats engineering/lab reporting, and Apple-run fitness or activity challenges with a first-party Apple Watch/Fitness/Workout/Health surface and a concrete participation or award action; a third-party app mentioned only as an eligible data source must not demote or redefine that Apple action. Comparison with AirPods, Android support, a third-party artist, or another product in body background likewise must not redefine the title-led first-party event. Historical `How X became Y` service retrospectives without a current launch, feature, policy, price, or availability action remain `weak` and must not bridge into a current service event.
- Service/content event identity must be concrete. A shared service name and broad release wording are never enough to merge events: keep different programs, films, seasons, charts, schedules, and catalog actions separate. A catalog-style article that only enumerates previously announced shows, films, seasons, or releases remains a `weak` roundup; it becomes eligible only when the title or opening lead establishes a new first-party announcement, schedule, trailer, premiere, renewal, availability, price, policy, or other concrete service action. When one source article announces or previews multiple specifically named works, project one event per named work/action while retaining the shared source URL and compatible facts. An ending or final-season action conflicts with an unrelated new-project or premiere action; announcement and premiere wording may describe the same work only when the current work title or another concrete content identity agrees. Quoted prior works used only as director, cast, author, or award background must not define the current content event.
- Apple Intelligence, Siri, and on-device AI model stories are software/system news when Apple is directly exploring, evaluating, negotiating for, adopting, compressing, enabling, pricing, or setting usage allowances for those capabilities. An attributed Apple executive statement about first-party AI compute costs, subscription tiers, or undecided usage limits remains a direct Apple service-policy event even when the article discusses Google Gemini or another provider as implementation context. Treat the action as direct Apple news whether the wording is `Apple is in talks with X`, `X is in talks with Apple`, or the equivalent Chinese subject order; a third-party model release that only runs on Apple devices remains `weak` unless the title or lead also reports that direct Apple evaluation or negotiation. Merge multilingual coverage only when it shares the same Apple action, model technology, startup/project, local-device capability, or attributed pricing/allowance decision; do not merge these AI model stories with unrelated Apple Wallet, App Store, OS guide, or generic AI market items merely because they mention iPhone, Apple, AI, or a third-party model name.
- Apple TV app or iTunes Store purchased-content entitlement changes, such as free 4K upgrades for previously purchased TV shows, are Apple service/content news. Classify them under software/services, not hardware, unless the article is actually about an Apple TV device, remote, chip, or tvOS hardware-adjacent feature. Apple TV+ awards/nomination results and purchased-content entitlement upgrades are separate service actions; do not merge them merely because both mention Apple TV or Apple services.
- Apple developer tools and ecosystem infrastructure, including Xcode, TestFlight, Swift, package indexes, package signing/identity, documentation hosting, and open-source developer projects when Apple changes support, integrates them, or they join Apple while continuing to affect Apple platform development. A developer's iOS/macOS experience is biographical context rather than an Apple action: an independently built app, game, AI experiment, contest entry, or coding project remains `weak` unless the article reports a concrete Apple platform, policy, tool, award, acquisition, approval, or interoperability action.
- Apple browser and developer-tool infrastructure such as Safari Technology Preview, WebKit updates, Safari MCP Server, WebDriver, debugging APIs, or coding-agent inspection/debug features are software/system news when Apple ships or documents the capability. Do not exclude Safari Technology Preview because `preview` looks like `review`.
- Apple first-party professional and creative app updates are software/system news when Apple changes the app or workflow, including Final Cut Camera, Final Cut Pro, Logic Pro, Pixelmator, Motion, Compressor, and Apple Creator Studio related updates. Keep them separate from iPhone camera hardware rumors, camera patents, image-sensor designs, or device camera leaks unless a source explicitly reports one combined Apple app/hardware change.
- Hardware and accessories: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, AirPods, Vision Pro, HomePod, official accessories, Apple silicon, modems, displays, cameras, and supply-chain production shifts. Include Apple official accessory availability changes such as Apple Store removal, regional unavailability, discontinuation, suspected discontinuation, Apple Online Store downtime/update status tied to pricing, product pages, launches, or promotions, official Apple Store/Apple online store availability for third-party accessories, and Apple Certified Refurbished / official refurbished-store availability or pricing changes for Apple products; treat them as hardware/product news even if the article mentions third-party headset, glasses, or broader market context. A material payment, checkout, or financing option newly usable while buying Apple products on Apple's own online retail surface is likewise a direct retail/sales action even when the payment provider announces it; do not confuse that checkout change with a third-party app merely arriving on an Apple platform. Apple-confirmed carrier-financing, device-payment, SIM/network-lock, or unlock-policy changes for iPhone are also direct hardware/sales-policy news when the title or lead cites an Apple policy, FAQ, or confirmation; ordinary unlocking advice, carrier promotions, and user workarounds remain `weak`.
- Apple hardware launch, roadmap, or rumor stories remain hardware/product news when the subject is an Apple device and the material details are design, display, camera, chip, modem, sensor, battery, Touch ID/Face ID, SIM/eSIM configuration, storage, or release timing, even if the article also discusses health, Apple Intelligence, Siri, OS support, carrier/telecom service status, or other software- or service-adjacent capabilities. Apple product price increases, component-cost pressure, memory/storage shortages, supply-cost pass-through stories, Apple explanations or responses to those price changes, and Apple product/supplier data-leak stories involving schematics, data sheets, source files, modem files, board designs, factory breaches, or dark-web file leaks are also hardware/product news, not software/system news. Treat a headline's concrete model identity as a negative boundary rather than merge proof: iPhone Air, base iPhone, `e`, Pro/Pro Max, Ultra, and other explicitly different variants must not merge only because they share a generation, year, roadmap, price, memory, or launch window; matching model names still require the same concrete action or attributed report. A shared named analyst, researcher, publication, filing, or report attribution plus the same model and compatible concrete action is strong cross-language duplicate evidence, including compact Chinese titles that omit spaces around an English model name; the attribution alone must not bridge different models, institutions, intents, or actions, and a broad unattributed industry or supply story must stay outside that attributed report cohort unless its title identifies the same product and action.
- Apple retail device deployments are hardware/product news when Apple equips store employees with iPhone, iPad, payment terminals, card readers, or replacement store equipment. A related Apple Pay, Tap to Pay, NFC, or later OS feature mentioned as context does not move the retail hardware action into software/system news.
- Concrete institutional adoption of first-party Apple hardware is hardware/product news when the title and lead make Apple or an Apple device the subject and report measured deployment, migration, replacement, or purchasing by schools, universities, enterprises, or other organizations. Windows, Chromebook, Android, or another displaced platform is comparison context in that case, not the primary subject; require attributable Apple or institutional reporting plus concrete adoption evidence so ordinary competitor comparisons remain weak.
- Design inspiration and functional analogies do not establish a second hardware action. Preserve the comparison in the owning product's facts, but do not project the referenced device into a current launch or roadmap. A separately stated action for that device still retains its own subject and may be projected.
- Apple hardware supply-chain and display-panel reports are hardware/product news when they describe concrete Apple product orders, supplier allocation, mass production, RFI, panel planning, component supply, shipment volume, or Apple negotiating a supplier purchase-price reduction for iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Vision Pro, or other Apple hardware. Cross-source reports about entering mass production, a production ramp, or the same display-price negotiation may merge only when the title/lead names the same explicit product generation, component scope, suppliers, and concrete production/procurement action; different generations or a panel/component order versus whole-device production remain separate. Keep broad multi-vendor market-share reports, title-led sales performance for one named Apple product, Apple quarterly financial results, material stock reactions, and product supply constraints as separate actions; body background about another one of these actions must not redefine or bridge the title-led event. Keep a forecast or revision for one named Apple product's production/shipments separate from a broader market-share or multi-vendor shipment report unless both sources measure the same product, period, metric, and action. Government pressure, incentives, tariff talks, or negotiations that directly concern Apple adopting a named chip foundry or fabrication service are also hardware supply-chain actions; merge them with another report only when the named foundry and Apple sourcing/fabrication action are the same. Supplier or competitor names such as Samsung, LG, BOE, or 京东方 are context in these stories and must not by themselves demote the event.
- A measured category forecast remains eligible hardware-market news when the title and lead identify a named Apple product as a material driver and provide a concrete shipment, sales, share, or growth metric. Keep it separate from worldwide component shortages, sold-out industry capacity, supplier pricing commentary, and multi-brand procurement stories unless those sources report the same Apple product, metric, and action. A direct Apple negotiation with a named supplier remains strong even when the supplier rejects Apple's requested price; a supplier's general pricing policy or commitments to other vendors is a separate weak industry event unless it independently reports the Apple negotiation.
- A hardware market-result event requires a concrete device market and reporting period, plus a locally attributed Apple shipment, unit-sales, order-share, revenue-share, or market-share result. Vendor-neutral device-market reports may establish that result in the body or table, as described under detail admission above. A research-firm name, Apple product name, percentage, user count, spending amount, content-consumption pattern, preference survey, or comparison alone is not a hardware-market action. Keep a single-product production or shipment forecast separate from an observed market result unless the sources measure the same product, period, metric, and action.
- Government tax exemptions, equipment-duty relief, manufacturing incentives, or similar measures are hardware/supply-chain news when the title or lead directly ties them to Apple production or Apple contract manufacturers. Terms such as `tax break`, `优惠`, `豁免`, or `减免` describe industrial policy in this context and must not trigger the routine retail-deal exclusion; merge reports only when the country, covered manufacturing equipment or production scope, policy action, and effective period agree.
- Determine hardware-market identity from the title and opening lead before using body metrics. A concrete Apple-product shortage, backorder, constrained inventory, or delayed shipment with current Apple Store or retail availability evidence is a direct hardware event and may merge across sources only when the product and supply action match. A broad component or SoC market report remains `weak` when Apple appears only as a component customer or background metric; do not let that body metric bridge it to a title-led iPhone component-cost/BOM analysis. Likewise, an explicit retailer discount/deal stays `weak` and separate from a defect, shortage, roadmap, or price-increase event for the same model. Apple smart-glasses health/fitness-platform reporting may merge across job-listing, attributed-report, and translated coverage when all sources identify the same Apple glasses health action; generic Vision Pro, Apple TV, or Fitness+ background is not separate merge proof.
- Reports about the same product's manufacturing hurdles, low yield, production hiccups, final production adjustments, or resulting launch uncertainty may merge across wording and language. Countries or regions mentioned only as incidental supply-chain background are not event boundaries.
- OLED or display-panel stories led by a competitor product or non-Apple customer, such as a Samsung Galaxy panel order, should remain `weak` when Apple appears only as a prior lost order, comparison, or market background. Keep them separate from direct Apple panel-order or Apple product supply-chain events.
- Display-panel and OLED supply-chain stories must keep their product scope. Broad multi-product Apple panel allocation or order stories, single-product foldable-iPhone panel production stories, and foldable-iPhone launch/timing or hinge-production stories are separate unless the sources clearly describe the same Apple panel order/action for the same product scope.
- Single-product display-spec reports may merge across different headline angles when they cite the same attributed report and repeat the same concrete panel, production, and launch facts. Keep a multi-product panel allocation or supplier roadmap separate even when its body mentions that single product, and do not let comparison products in the body redefine the title's product scope.
- Health/accessibility features and country availability changes.
- Acquisition-strategy commentary needs a concrete transaction or newly disclosed transaction fact to qualify. General statements about repeated acquisitions and their inferred purpose do not establish a new Apple action. Preserve newly disclosed older deals, current negotiations and named asset purchases; neither an old transaction date nor surrounding commentary alone excludes them. Component procurement is a supply relationship, not a company acquisition; bind the acquired party separately from the seller.
- Company actions tied to Apple products/services, including legal, regulatory, donations, partnerships, executive changes, retail/product availability, office leases or property acquisitions, physical incidents at Apple-owned or Apple-leased facilities, and regional business moves. Direct Apple office/real-estate expansion and a fire, evacuation, outage, flood, explosion, or comparable physical incident at a named Apple facility are eligible company news under hardware/products; generic real-estate background, vacancy data, emergency-service background, or another tenant must not override the Apple facility action, and reports merge only when they identify the same facility and incident. Direct analyst upgrades/downgrades and Apple/AAPL rating or price-target changes are eligible company/market actions, including standard `raises/cuts/lifts AAPL target` headline wording, but must stay separate from shipment/share reports, product pricing, stock-price moves, and another institution's rating/target action even when the notes cite the same Apple results or product metrics. Merge target/rating reports only when the named institution and concrete action agree; a target amount, `AAPL`, analyst wording, or publication date alone is insufficient. An analyst's unchanged bullish/bearish stance, earnings preview without a new rating/target action, or valuation commentary remains `weak` when it contains no new rating/target action, material Apple stock move, or newly reported Apple pricing/business action. Classify a legal case under hardware/products when its concrete subject is unreleased physical hardware, product designs, device roadmaps, hardware engineers, or hardware trade secrets; ordinary App Store, platform-policy, privacy, or software litigation remains under software/systems. A legal story whose title and opening sentences concern a separate dispute between non-Apple parties remains `weak` when a later paragraph only compares that case with an Apple lawsuit; the later Apple reference must not pull the article into the Apple case cluster. Analysis of an existing Apple lawsuit remains `weak` when it only speculates about why a person or party was omitted, might later participate, or could face discovery and reports no new filing, ruling, testimony, settlement, or confirmed procedural action.
- Apple donation, relief, and disaster-aid commitments are direct company actions when Apple or an Apple executive is the actor. Terms such as donating, donation, relief efforts, aid, or equivalent Chinese phrasing should be treated as action anchors rather than ignored as generic background. Preserve affected regions, casualties or damage stated as context, prior relief actions, and any disclosed or explicitly undisclosed aid details, but do not import unrelated succession, leadership, social-account, or communications commentary from the same source into the relief event's key facts.
- Apple Books, App Store, and other Apple platform-trust stories are eligible when the article's lead subject is an Apple platform and the concrete issue is fraud, scams, AI-generated knockoffs, impersonation, copyright infringement, app/book review failure, platform enforcement, or equivalent trust/safety problems. A material title/lead-supported removal from or restoration to the App Store is a direct `strong` platform-enforcement action even when the headline uses commentary or question framing; preserve the current removal/restoration reason and outcome as required facts, but not historical enforcement examples. Payment restoration, a first-party utility retirement, an Apple acquisition whose body mentions a delisting, and historical App Store background are separate actions and must not be reclassified as app delisting/restoration. Do not exclude platform-trust stories merely because a background paragraph mentions Amazon or another store; keep ordinary retailer, affiliate, and deal exclusions intact for non-platform-trust stories.
- Apple strategic transaction, merger, acquisition, or buyout discussions when Apple is a direct party and the report provides a new interview, filing, source claim, or other concrete disclosure. Do not include generic speculation or old rumor recaps without a current substantive update.
- Apple leadership, design-team, management-structure, product-decision authority, and Apple executive attendance at material industry, investor, government, or closed-door business events where Apple is the subject. Company-wide product-development, release-calendar and management reorganization is hardware/products company news; classify separately bound workforce or business actions by their own functional team, retaining software/services decisions under software/systems. Competitor, partner, investor, or media-company names in an attendee list must not hard-exclude those Apple company stories. Former-Apple staff, startup, or third-party company background must not downgrade direct Apple company stories, but stories whose lead subject is a third-party company, founder, model, product, or market and that mention prior Apple employment or historical Apple-chip work only as biography remain `weak`; a courtesy visit or gift to Apple executives also remains `weak` unless it announces a concrete partnership, investment, product, platform, policy, or management action. Opinion columns, recaps, or commentary without new reporting remain `weak`. In Chinese text, treat former-Apple references as person/role phrases such as `前苹果员工`, `前苹果工程师`, or `苹果前高管`; do not infer former-staff context from bare substrings inside ordinary phrases such as `目前苹果官网` or `此前苹果产品`.
- Classify a concrete Apple leadership hiring, rehire, appointment, or succession action under hardware/products when the role and stated responsibility are explicitly hardware engineering, product delivery, product management, or hardware-team organization. Historical product mentions or a retirement year are supporting context, not a separate product event or executive-tenure retrospective; merge cross-source reports only when the people, personnel action, and organizational role agree.
- Apple-specific regional regulation remains strong when a regulator, court, or government body acts directly on Apple platform behavior, such as local app/search preinstall requirements, App Store rules, payments, antitrust fines, warnings, compliance deadlines, export controls, authorization to access regulated chips, servers, data-center equipment, and other controlled technology, a tariff exemption tied to a concrete Apple chip-production commitment, or a government trade investigation, tariff threat, or retaliation action explicitly responding to penalties imposed on Apple. Merge multilingual coverage only when the regulator/government, region, affected Apple capability or penalty, and concrete approval/restriction/retaliation action are the same. Hardware/foundry and regulation angles of the same tariff exemption may merge when they share Apple, the same named chip supplier or foundry, and the same production commitment/exemption action; do not let generic chip or data-center background demote a direct government action on Apple, and keep unrelated legal/regulatory items separate.
- WWDC-related events when they pass the normal relevance and time-window rules, including keynote, schedule, OS-preview, developer, Apple executive, attendee gift/swag, badge, sticker, Developer app, mascot, and official conference-material stories. Do not downgrade or omit them merely because they look light, promotional, or less important than product rumors.
- Current reporting about concrete Apple event operations is company/product news when it describes staff support, attendee handling, presentation production, prerecorded segments, live stage elements, or a mixed live/recorded format for the same named event. Merge such reports only when they describe the same concrete preparation action and object, including compatible sparse or translated reporting of that action. Staff recruitment or a support lottery, presentation format, store maintenance, developer resources, and preorders remain separate actions even for the same named event. Historical date-pattern analysis, countdowns, and product-preview roundups remain weak and must not bridge into an operational action.
- Official Apple product campaigns are direct company/product actions when the title or lead establishes that Apple published or promoted the campaign, identifies the Apple product, and names the campaign or format, such as an official `Shot on iPhone` release. Keep ordinary creator projects, third-party videos, reviews, and generic `shot on iPhone` usage stories `weak` when they lack that Apple publication or campaign action.
- Apple research where Apple publishes, presents, previews, or showcases papers/studies at a recognized technical or academic venue, requiring anchors such as research, paper, study, CVPR, computer vision, or equivalent Chinese terms; generic AI strategy, WWDC positioning, third-party academic work, or social-impact studies that merely use iPhone/Apple as the study object are not enough by themselves. Include medical or health research where Apple Watch, Apple Health, ResearchKit, or Apple-collected health/activity/sensor data is central to a newly published or materially updated study, requiring both an Apple health data/product anchor and a research/study anchor. Also retain peer-reviewed applied research when a named first-party Apple product is the tested intervention or primary operational tool and the report provides measured outcome data, such as time, accuracy, workload, or complication rates; keep generic correlation studies, usage anecdotes, sleep tips, wellness guides, and app advice excluded.
- Apple messaging-platform, Apple Wallet, and third-party Apple-platform availability stories when they include a concrete Apple platform/action anchor: Apple Messages for Business, iMessage, or Messages capability changes must combine a concrete Apple messaging-platform anchor, an AI agent/assistant or similar capability anchor, and a positive approval, integration, or availability action, and must not be promoted when that context is negated, such as "not integrated with iMessage" or "没有接入 iMessage". Apple Wallet car-key code clues, partner automaker support, or platform availability changes are Apple platform news when the action is Wallet/car-key support rather than the automaker itself. Third-party app, game, service, carrier offer, or utility availability on an Apple platform or App Store remains `weak` unless Apple approval, Apple platform policy, Apple interoperability, or a direct Apple product/service change makes it stronger; routine third-party app/game updates, service-status or rectification notices that merely describe use on an iPhone/Apple phone, and stories that mention iOS, watchOS, CarPlay, Control Center, Lock Screen, Siri Shortcuts, Apple Watch, HarmonyOS/WeChat, Android/HarmonyOS/PC cross-progression, or platform compatibility are still `weak` when the title subject and action belong to that third-party app or game rather than Apple.
- A concrete third-party rollout that directly connects to, reads, imports, or synchronizes a first-party Apple data platform or API such as Apple Health or HealthKit is an `ecosystem` event when the title establishes both the integration and current rollout. Privacy commentary or risk analysis without a new rollout remains `weak` and must not be absorbed as a source for that rollout.
- First-party Apple OS, system-app, support-document, Siri/Apple Intelligence, built-in-app, interface, widget, or Smart Stack changes remain software/system events when the title and lead make an Apple platform or first-party component the subject. This classification follows the changed object: a Widget Gallery, widget rotation/mirroring, or other first-party interface capability remains software/system news even when it is discovered in code for an unreleased Apple device such as a Home Hub. Apple Intelligence or Siri features that let Siri read, reason over, or act on third-party app data are first-party OS/Siri events when Apple is changing the platform behavior; routine third-party app updates remain `weak`. Title/lead subject and action take precedence over later third-party examples, comparisons, related links, or opinion framing, but personal feature walkthroughs, favorite-feature videos, and recaps without a new standalone Apple action remain `weak`. Do not reclassify first-party OS/App/Siri stories as hardware, Wallet, App Store, or third-party ecosystem stories merely because the body mentions Apple TV hardware, Apple Wallet, App Store, Google calendars, third-party calendars, or other background components.
- A versioned OS article remains eligible when its title names one current Apple OS train, explicitly reports added or changed features, and the detail page supplies concrete system behavior, settings, or built-in-app facts. Experiential wording such as `I have been using` or a short numbered feature count must not by itself demote that direct feature report; generic favorite-feature lists, tutorials, and recaps without a new current-version change remain weak.
- Direct Apple product, supplier, certification, or government-investigation data leaks remain strong when the main subject is unreleased Apple product data, Apple supplier files, dark-web disclosure, or regulator/security-team investigation of an Apple supplier breach. Non-Apple processor reviews, phone launches, design references, benchmark comparisons, or multi-vendor market/roadmap stories remain `weak` when Apple products, Apple chip-like names, iPhone colors, MacBook performance, or Apple pricing are used mainly as comparison context.

- Bind the current source-owned action before using related products or historical context. A versioned first-party app update belongs to that app, including translated labels and title-adjacent version numbers; explicit version conflicts remain distinct. Publication of release notes or technical drawings belongs to the document action, and an OS capability found in code belongs to software even when its host device is unreleased. Device mentions in a document inventory cannot join it to a firmware update. A release-note headline whose lead announces an actual OS/RC push retains that release action; a device named as the host for a Siri Remote change does not replace the accessory as the subject. Keep model generations and app builds attached to their own subject; an OS compatibility version is not an app build.
- Project multi-device image/code disclosures by locally supported product claims and preserve unassigned source spans for review. Shared code provenance does not make different products one event; a device disclosure remains distinct from an announced release or a separate OS capability. Current first-party device data and versioned system capability reports do not become weak solely because the publisher uses a guide, image or introductory angle. Ordinary automaker OTA compatibility, personal viewing commentary and historical interviews do not establish a new Apple action.

Exclude:

- Treat `reasons to buy now instead of waiting` articles and list-style reasons for current users to upgrade as buying advice unless the article independently reports a new Apple release, action, or material fact.
- Deals, coupons, affiliate roundups, routine buying guides, wishlist/opinion pieces, `what to expect` / `here's what we know` product outlooks, multi-product or multi-brand launch predictions, and list-style rumor or feature recaps without new reporting, including `rumor reality check`, `传闻汇总`, `爆料汇总`, and summaries of already exposed upgrades, should remain `weak`; the same applies to first-person Apple fleet/product-usage columns, single-user durability, repair, survival, or workaround anecdotes, weekly crime blotters/roundups where Apple devices are merely stolen goods or tracking evidence, personal favorite-feature/video walkthroughs, viral AI-generated Apple-product photo/image debunks, independently built novelty projects that merely use Apple-device sensors or controls, and how-to/tutorial, personal settings, device-usage, or install-preparation content without a new Apple release/action. Settings advice that starts from a user symptom or recommends toggling an existing option remains a tutorial unless the title and lead independently establish a current Apple OS, built-in-app, or support-policy change; a user's repair outcome, official-repair quote, or third-party workaround likewise does not become an Apple action merely because an Apple device is central to the anecdote. A title phrase such as `what to expect` is not by itself a weak signal when the article is a current, attributed, single-product report and its lead supplies concrete new testing, launch, design, availability, or cancellation facts; classify and merge that report by its Apple product and action rather than by the title format. Conversely, an editorial inference that explicitly says no new report or evidence exists, and merely projects a future Apple product from an unchanged design, historical cadence, or prior roadmap, remains `weak`; translations or rewrites citing that same inference do not elevate it into an independently sourced report. List formatting alone is not proof of a non-news roundup: a current-window report about a direct Apple OS, built-in app, platform feature, or performance change remains eligible when it carries a new release/beta action or concrete new feature/performance data, even when the title says `full list`, `ways`, or an item count. Articles whose lead is "how to get ready", "when you can install", "which product is right for you", "we need Apple to add", "with these settings", "how I use", "before you buy", or "before you pull the trigger", setup steps, beta-preparation steps, install instructions, Chinese "新品前瞻 / 发布会前瞻 / 产品前瞻 / 新品展望 / 怎么办 / 打开这个功能 / 提前开启 / 如何找回 / 善用 / 儿童手机" style preview or device-usage content, or lost-device safety advice should remain `weak` unless the article itself reports a new release, new version availability, a material Apple platform change, or independently sourced new facts. AI-generated knockoffs, fraud, impersonation, or copyright failures on Apple Books/App Store remain eligible platform-trust news; the weak rule here applies only when the article's action is debunking a viral fake product image and its Apple specifications are recycled background.
- Treat a plural-rumor digest or an unsourced multi-product release calendar as `weak` when it only reorganizes older claims, even if the title names a future Apple model, item count, launch window, or many concrete specifications. Preserve a current attributed report when its title or opening lead identifies the reporter, analyst, filing, research note, supply-chain source, or other new evidence. Likewise, when a headline uses an unattributed Apple price forecast as its opening hook but pivots to a named non-Apple company's broad industry shortage, capacity, or market forecast, classify the article by that non-Apple primary action; do not promote it merely because the hook mentions an iPhone price. A named current Apple price report or a direct Apple pricing action remains eligible.
- Opinion-only commentary or product analysis without new Apple facts. Apple product commentary should remain `weak` when the title and lead are about why a rumor is good/bad news, what it means, or the author's take, even if the body references earlier hardware rumors; guess/speculation framing remains `weak` even when it proposes a concrete price or other number, unless the title or opening lead attributes a current Apple action or new report. Direct reports, filings, support documents, Apple confirmations, or source-backed new product facts remain eligible. Commentary-style framing such as `here's why` must not demote a current Apple hardware roadmap when the opening lead attributes a concrete launch, cancellation, generation transition, production, or timing claim to a named report or source; the same framing without current attribution or a concrete new action remains `weak`.
- Podcast/newsletter episodes unless the article adds new reporting.
- Reviews and hands-on articles unless they disclose a new product fact or policy.
- Simple rewrites or reposts without new details.

Do not let exclusion rules hide a clear Apple-subject event. Competitor, third-party, or partner names in the body should not by themselves weaken or exclude a story whose main subject and lead action is about Apple OS/platform changes, Apple services, Apple official accessories, Apple legal actions, Apple developer tools, or Apple ecosystem interoperability, even when the article supplies Google, Samsung, Meta, Amazon, or other market background. Keep the existing exclusions for routine deals, affiliate roundups, and non-Apple product stories, but include official Apple Card, Apple Pay, App Store, Apple services, and Apple retail/customer acquisition promotions when they reveal a new or materially changed Apple business offer; these are service/business news, not ordinary affiliate deals, and summaries should include value, eligibility, timing, source confidence, and unresolved terms.

Do not promote third-party or competitor comparison/marketing stories just because they mention an Apple product, Apple chip-like model string, Apple-like ambiguous term, benchmark result, or macOS/iOS compatibility. Determine the action owner from the title and lead regardless of word order: a non-Apple vendor launching or positioning its own product remains the owner when `rival`, `competitor`, `alternative`, `竞品`, `对手`, or equivalent comparison wording appears either before or after the Apple product name. Bare model names such as `M3` or `C1` only count as Apple anchors when the surrounding context clearly identifies Apple silicon or an Apple product; `Swift` only counts as an Apple anchor when the surrounding context clearly identifies Apple's programming language, developer tools, or Apple platform development; `Safari` only counts as an Apple anchor in browser or Apple platform context, not vehicle names such as Safari EV; Chinese `库克` only counts when it clearly refers to Tim Cook or Apple leadership, not place names. Likewise, bare words such as `Vision Pro` count only when the surrounding context identifies Apple's headset, visionOS, spatial-computing platform, app, or accessory; a third-party product model name that merely contains those words is not an Apple anchor. Rival marketing, buying advice, `vs`/comparison pieces, multi-brand activation or sales comparisons framed around one product beating several rivals or their combined total, benchmark/performance comparisons where Apple chips or products are only reference points, generic cross-platform compatibility/configuration lists, subjective hands-on reviews without a quantitative Apple-hardware measurement, weekly recaps, generic consumer-electronics safety stories, third-party device-management service launches, law-enforcement/surveillance stories that merely track Apple devices, non-Apple OS vulnerability-count/statistics stories that only compare disclosure totals with Apple, non-Apple vendor responses to an Apple product such as PC makers rebranding chips to compete with a MacBook, and third-party XR or smart-glasses stories using Apple, iPhone, or Vision Pro mainly as market comparison should remain `weak` unless they contain a new Apple action, a clearly attributed Apple-specific research metric in the title/lead, direct Apple vulnerability/fix or policy/platform change, Apple Vision product-roadmap news, or concrete Apple ecosystem interoperability. These comparison items must not merge into direct Apple hardware, pricing, security, or supply-chain events; a competitor product launch that compares its timing, thickness, storage, display, or foldable roadmap with future iPhones remains separate from Apple hardware rumors unless the title and lead action are actually about Apple. Product and action evidence in the title and opening lead controls identity; a later comparison, related example, affiliate link, or speculative adjacent Apple device must not replace the title product or create a second event facet.

Generic strong-event promotion requires title-led direct Apple scope, or an explicit first-party title/lead anchor paired with a structured subject or action. An Apple term found only in body background, a historical product example, an unrelated comparison, or another lexical collision must not turn a non-Apple article into an Apple security, hardware, regulatory, or service event; keep a still-reviewable match `weak`, or exclude it when no direct Apple action remains.

Keep stories `weak` when the current action belongs to someone other than Apple and the Apple connection is only incidental: a personal rescue or usage anecdote about an existing feature, a carrier/network outage that merely puts iPhones into SOS mode, a crime or fraud case where Apple devices are purchased or stolen goods, a third-party financial service whose card can be added to Apple Wallet/Apple Pay, or a third-party accessory review/promotion. A new Apple feature, policy, platform restriction, official program, first-party accessory, or confirmed company response remains eligible; title/lead evidence for that Apple action must take precedence over accessory or recommendation terms found only in article-tail context.

When the title's subject is a non-Apple product launch, non-Apple AI/app/service expansion, Android-user survey, or broad OS/desktop market-share report, keep it `weak` even if the body compares timing, availability, compatibility, or market impact with iPhone, iOS, macOS, Apple Intelligence, or future Apple products. Preserve the existing `ecosystem` path only when the third-party platform update directly improves interoperability, pairing, connection reliability, audio behavior, or compatibility for Apple first-party devices.

Broad AI phone, AI PC, LLM, or consumer-market commentary that uses iPhone, Apple Intelligence, or Apple upgrade intent as one example remains `weak` unless the title/lead is directly about a new Apple platform, product, service, policy, or sourced Apple market result. Do not promote a generic AI-device market piece merely because it includes Apple Intelligence survey percentages.

Retail/deal filtering should match real retailer, discount, coupon, buying-guide, or affiliate context, not substrings inside supplier names or product-roadmap terms. A retailer-named title with a concrete sale price and a lead describing discounts, low prices, affiliate links, a deals roundup, or a deals newsletter is a routine deal even when the title omits the literal words `deal`, `sale`, or `discount`. For example, `京东方` / BOE is a display supplier and must not be interpreted as `京东` retail context when the article is about Apple OLED panel orders or production. Direct Apple-subject hardware pricing, component-cost, market-forecast, and official Apple retail/customer-acquisition promotion stories should still reach detail-page review even when listing text uses words such as price, increase, forecast, promotion, 涨价, 提高, 上涨, 预估, 返校季, or 促销; routine third-party retailer discounts, coupons, buying guides, and affiliate deal posts remain excluded.

For non-Apple-subject stories, Apple price, supply, safety, or market references in the body are background unless the headline or focused roundup subitem is itself about Apple, an Apple product, or a direct Apple business action. Broad multi-vendor product, service, music, media, or platform market reports should remain `weak` when Apple is only one listed vendor and the story lacks Apple-specific shipment, share, growth, ranking, pricing, or customer-loyalty detail; Apple- or iPhone-specific market reports with concrete Apple data in the title or lead remain eligible even if later body text contains generic words such as client, service, release, or another vendor. These market metrics concern observed purchases, retention or switching, shipments, share, growth and pricing. Subjective satisfaction scores, preference polls and purchase intentions remain weak; an Apple-specific ranking or percentage alone does not turn them into market results or device measurements. An eligible Apple-specific metric must bind the Apple subject and numeric result in the same title, lead, sentence, table row, or compact clause; a nearby component-capacity, panel-shipment, competitor, or broad-market number must not stand in for an Apple product metric. A global company ranking or market-cap report led by another company remains `weak` when Apple is only the company being surpassed or compared, unless the report supplies a concrete Apple-specific stock move or product-market metric. Merge market reports only when they share the same Apple product family, reporting period, and compatible shipment/share metric; a competitor-led regional survey that merely lists iPhone as a comparison must stay separate. This prevents competitor or generic-market articles from becoming Apple events solely because a later paragraph compares or mentions Apple.

Market-report identity must be compatible across geographic scope, product segment, and reporting period before shared metrics can support a merge. The named research firm is attribution evidence rather than a hard boundary when independent firms report the same Apple metric for the same scope and period. When both sources explicitly identify a scope dimension and the values conflict, keep the reports separate; publication dates, calendar years, generic shipment/share words, and coincidentally repeated numbers are not report identity.

Region labels require a country/region name or a bounded abbreviation. An English word suffix or the pronoun “us” is not a United States marker; retain explicit constituent-country references such as England under their country label.

A newly reported regional price series for specific Apple devices is a product-market report even when based on observed reseller or user prices. Distinguish that market measurement from a retailer's sale, coupon or affiliate offer. Bind a price action to its clause's actor: an industry or component noun appearing earlier in the headline does not make a separate Apple price report third-party news. A competitor's price action with Apple only as background remains weak, whether or not it explicitly says it follows Apple. Keep the model/capacity rows, price ranges and eligibility, attributing observations and store variation; do not recast them as official Apple pricing or a newly announced tax policy. Incidental shopping advice may be removed without excluding the price report.

For non-Apple-subject chip, phone, or hardware-roadmap stories, Apple A-series chips, iPhone Air, iPhone 18, or Apple chip references in a multi-vendor comparison are background unless the title or focused subitem is directly about Apple. Stories led by MediaTek, Qualcomm, Huawei, Xiaomi, Vivo, Oppo, Android phones, or generic chip launches should remain `weak` when Apple is only one peer in a list such as Qualcomm/Apple/MediaTek or an industry timing comparison.

Third-party security software, cleaner, VPN, firewall, subscription, or discount/promo articles for Mac/iOS users should remain `weak` when they are product promotions, buying advice, or compatibility pitches. Do not upgrade them to Apple security/privacy events merely because the article mentions malware, antivirus, macOS compatibility, or protecting a Mac; keep direct Apple security features, Apple support documents, vulnerabilities, patches, and platform protections eligible.

Marketing language should not override material first-party hardware news. Apple or Beats unreleased hardware sightings are eligible hardware/product events when the report includes concrete product details such as model or product-line clues, FCC/certification records, design or color changes, availability or release timing, or other hardware facts. Pure ads, celebrity campaigns, social posts, sponsorships, and commercial promos that lack those product details remain excluded or `weak`.

Routine third-party accessory or consumer-electronics stories that only mention Apple-platform compatibility, such as a HomeKit/Apple Home device, dock, charger, hub, monitor, keyboard, storage enclosure, external drive, or other accessory supporting macOS, iOS, iPhone, iPad, or Mac, should remain `weak` unless the article's subject is Apple/Beats hardware, an official Apple accessory, an unreleased first-party hardware detail, Apple Store availability, Apple certification/policy, or a direct Apple platform action. HomeKit compatibility is not itself a first-party Apple data-integration action; classify it from the title action owner rather than from an accessory-name allow/deny list. Treat an item as a physical accessory only when the title or lead identifies an actual accessory/product phrase; ambiguous words such as legal `case`, Wallet, card, support, or cover in non-product context must not create an accessory identity. Apple-pushed AirPods/Beats firmware releases and official Apple/Beats accessory availability changes are direct Apple events, not third-party accessory compatibility stories. Do not let compatibility mentions merge with unrelated OS, security, or support-document events.

Third-party reference, visualization, explainer, preservation, showcase, retrospective, and personal hardware-modification projects about Apple products, including demonstrations that port Apple software to non-Apple hardware, such as interactive device timelines, time capsules, "how a component is made" videos, self-upgraded MacBooks, or community repair/mod stories, should remain `weak` unless Apple itself released the project or the article reports a new Apple product, system, service, policy, repair program, or developer-platform action, or a material quantitative Apple-device test meeting the measurement rule below. Non-Apple organizations responding to procurement, charity, donation, relief, PR, sponsorship, or operational controversies that merely involve buying or using Apple hardware should also remain `weak`, including articles whose Apple angle is only a previous Apple-computer purchase controversy; Apple hardware is context, not the subject. Third-party malware, ad, or scam stories should remain `weak` when the attack is led by a third-party app, ad platform, lookalike site, or security vendor report and does not disclose a new Apple platform vulnerability, Apple patch, Apple support guidance, or direct Apple security/privacy change.

Apple-published privacy campaigns, ads, or films that explain first-party iPhone, App Store, App Review, App Privacy Label, permission, or App Tracking Transparency protections are direct Apple security/privacy actions and should not be demoted merely because the story discusses third-party apps requesting data. Unreleased Apple wearable or AirPods stories found through OS beta code should be grouped by the specific product/action boundary: camera-equipped AirPods code clues, camera-equipped AirPods development suspension or delay, iPhone camera/sensor supplier leaks, and iPhone battery-capacity leaks are separate event families unless source review proves the same Apple action.

Apple chip process, node, or foundry rumors and denials are strong only when the title and lead action are about Apple silicon or an Apple foundry/manufacturing action, such as A-series/M-series chips, TSMC/Intel process nodes, or Apple chip production orders. A Google, Samsung, Qualcomm, MediaTek, Intel, AMD, or other competitor chip becoming the first customer for a process node remains `weak` when Apple is only the previous first-customer pattern or timing comparison. Do not promote ordinary competitor chip or PC processor reviews because they benchmark against MacBook, Apple silicon, or Apple-like product positioning.

A concrete third-party OS, kernel, graphics/compute driver, or standards-conformance advancement for Apple Silicon is an `ecosystem` software/system event when the title/lead identifies Apple hardware, the software stack, and the new support, compatibility, enablement, or certification action. Benchmarks without a material Apple-device finding and competitor-led comparisons remain `weak`, and unrelated compatibility stories must not merge merely because they mention Linux, drivers, or an M-series chip.

During fact extraction, remove article-tail service affiliate blocks, refurbished-product discount calls to action, sponsor/about-column boilerplate, article-end footer sections, related-link headline clusters, reader-feedback prompts, and pure UI-navigation steps before summarization. Examples include Apple service price rows such as `Apple Music - $10.99/mo after free trial`, short title-only related links, site breadcrumb/ICP boilerplate, same-site recommendation titles, 快科技 `【本文结束】` tails and `相关资讯`, 9to5Mac `Apple @ Work` sponsor/about copy, `Let us know in the comments`, and standalone instructions such as clicking an icon or choosing an item from a dropdown. These fragments can mention Apple products and numbers, but they should not create facts, classifications, or event merges; preserve substantive feature behavior and setting changes even when they explain what a user can select.

Promote a multi-row series into `must_include_facts` only when each row has a compact label-value structure, such as a device/model/region followed by its price, eligibility, support status, or other comparable value. A compact row may inherit its Apple product/service and list purpose from the immediately preceding substantive introduction, so rows such as plan names followed by old price, new price, and change amount must not be discarded merely because every row does not repeat the product name. Require the direct Apple article title, a compatible list introduction, and a numeric label-value or before/after shape; do not apply this inheritance to unrelated navigation, recommendations, or narrative prose. Model-generation labels such as M-series chip names inside a price/support row describe that row's product identity and must not turn it into a separate hardware-roadmap fact. Narrative paragraphs that happen to contain a colon and a number remain ordinary `key_facts`; they must not force historical anecdotes, examples, or unrelated chronology into the final brief. Explicitly labelled release-history appendices remain in captured evidence but do not become current required facts or review work merely because their rows contain dates and builds. Preserve current support/eligibility tables and an explicitly relevant prior build outside the appendix.

If a newly issued official Apple communication has no numeric or enumerated key facts, preserve its substantive narrative facts from the cleaned body instead of returning an empty fact list. Outcomes, explanations, and customer experiences presented in that official communication can matter without containing a number; subscription prompts and other boilerplate still do not qualify. This fallback does not turn a personal experience article into an official Apple communication.

When the detail page has a concrete Apple subject and action, treat that detail-page title and body as the event boundary. Listing/feed summaries, related-link context, or body paragraphs about another independent Apple action must not override the detail page's primary event, add incompatible `key_facts`, or influence merge tokens. Keep supporting subfacts for the same event family, such as chip, memory, battery, storage, or launch-timing details inside one hardware rumor, but split or discard background facts whose concrete subject/action belongs to a different event family such as OS signing status, Wallet identity rollout, tariff policy, product data leaks, component-cost forecasts, or foldable-iPhone mockups.

A newly published, attributed quantitative test with Apple hardware as its primary measured subject is eligible hardware news when it supplies a concrete method, configuration and material result. The tester may be an individual and may use an open-source tool or custom setup; neither requires an Apple announcement. Preserve all relevant measurements and their conditions, including stages that did not improve. This exception concerns measured device behavior, not a third-party tool launch, subjective upgrade recommendation, project showcase without such measured results or competitor-led benchmark using Apple only as a reference. Recognize locally bound measurements and their units across languages; an adjacent theoretical comparison or future expansion plan does not negate an actual test and must not inherit its eligibility.

Bind a focused device test to its tester, measured object and tested behavior. Equipment used to test an Apple device is a condition, not the report's primary subject. A dedicated test of current first-party operating behavior may instead qualify under the current-functionality rule without a numeric score. Keep its material method, environments and compared modes even when they contain no digits. Shared devices or specifications do not merge independently conducted tests or a focused test with a separate general review.

## Relevance Tiers

Keep candidate discovery broad. After detail-page verification, classify events by relevance tier:

- `strong`: direct Apple product, service, platform, retail, research, legal, regulatory, supply-chain, security, or company action.
- `ecosystem`: third-party or competitor action with a concrete Apple ecosystem effect, such as AirDrop/Quick Share interoperability, direct App Store platform compatibility, or a third-party OS/platform update that directly improves interoperability, pairing, connection reliability, audio behavior, or compatibility for Apple first-party devices such as AirPods, Beats, iPhone, iPad, or Apple Watch.
- `weak`: Apple is mainly used as context, comparison, or device target, such as routine third-party Vision Pro apps, competitor hardware comparisons, or unrelated non-Apple apps.
- Local AI apps, third-party utilities, or competitor services that merely run on iPhone, iPad, or Mac should remain `weak` unless the article describes a direct Apple platform change, Apple approval/integration, or concrete Apple ecosystem interoperability.
- Non-Apple apps or services that merely launch, return, update, or become compatible on iOS, iPadOS, macOS, watchOS, Apple Watch, iPhone, iPad, Mac, or App Store should remain `weak`, including Chinese "iOS 版上线 / 适配" style stories, unless Apple itself changed platform policy, approval, interoperability, or a first-party product/service behavior. Application control of existing camera hardware and ordinary phone-number ordering through Messages follow this rule; classify the operated software or service separately from its host device.
- Third-party apps/services that are simply listed on or compatible with an Apple platform should be retained for JSON traceability as `weak`, not promoted into the final brief by default.
- Third-party accessories or consumer-electronics products that only cite Apple-platform compatibility should also remain `weak`; keep them in JSON traceability, but do not promote them into the final brief as Apple hardware news unless a direct Apple/Beats or Apple-platform action is the subject.
- A vendor's new replacement head unit remains compatible third-party hardware even when marketed as an official upgrade for older vehicles. Distinguish that product launch from a firmware/platform update that changes an existing vehicle's CarPlay connection behavior; the latter may qualify as `ecosystem`. "Official" identifies the issuing vendor, not Apple by default.
- A completed institution-led deployment of an Apple device is not a routine third-party app update when the title and lead jointly prove real operational use plus a first-of-kind or regulatory milestone. Keep ordinary app launches and feature updates `weak`; promote only the measured deployment event, preserving the institution, device role, authorization, workflow change, and outcome details.
- Treat a newly published, attributed, title-led quantitative Apple company-performance result as a current market/company report rather than a generic leadership retrospective. The title must own a concrete Apple/AAPL metric and the lead must establish the reporting source and tenure context; later opinion does not erase the measured result, while an unquantified legacy assessment remains `weak`.

Final Markdown briefs include `strong` and `ecosystem` events. `weak` events may remain in JSON `deferred_events` for inspection so broad coverage is preserved without adding low-value items to the brief. Override a recorded weak reason only when the deferred article's own title or lead establishes a direct Apple action or a qualifying ecosystem operation under the rules above. A firmware/platform rollout that changes a specific Apple-device connection is distinct from a product's compatibility list; ordinary compatibility, a third-party app feature, analyst preview/opinion, or a causal effect on Apple is not enough by itself.

An aggregate event must not become `strong` merely because summaries from several individually `weak` articles mention Apple frequently when concatenated. Promotion requires at least one source whose own title and lead contain a direct Apple action or a concrete Apple ecosystem interoperability effect.

Treat title subject, action ownership, and content form as authoritative relevance inputs. A deal roundup, routine buying recommendation, third-party app spotlight, hands-on article, first-person design wish, future-support recap, user-built utility, or opinion/value analysis stays `weak` unless its own title and opening lead establish a new Apple release, first-party change, policy, filing, transaction, market result, or other concrete action. Conversely, a non-Apple title subject must not be promoted by Apple comparisons or background found later in the body; direct interoperability that changes support for an Apple first-party device is the narrow `ecosystem` exception. Keep these candidates discoverable for review rather than narrowing source discovery.

A current Apple employee leave notice remains a company action even when the benefit repeats a tradition. Retain the notice's extra day count, weekday range, eligible teams and alternative leave timing together, including when an internal email is rendered as ordinary paragraphs. An attributed device workload measurement remains hardware news when it supplies an actual measured result and testing conditions; a chip-led title may bind its device and workload from the opening, before competitor comparisons. Accessory examples do not own that result. For market reports, use the complete title and all source-owned measurement rows rather than a display shortlist, and retain the reporting period, region and per-store versus aggregate scope.

Apply path exclusions to complete URL path tokens or segments rather than arbitrary substrings. In particular, an editorial `review` path may be excluded while `preview`, including Safari Technology Preview release URLs, must remain discoverable. After detail parsing, use the title and opening lead to determine the primary actor and action; a third-party app, competitor product, opinion poll, or comparison must not become strong because Apple appears only in body background, while a direct iCloud+ entitlement, Apple official refurbished-store listing, or first-party hardware roadmap must not be weakened by incidental third-party names later in the article.

## Event-Level Merge Rules

Order reductions, supplier responses, equity-price changes and device-sales measurements have separate actors or objects. Background component costs do not determine those reports' identity or relevance. Match the same order operation across languages while preserving component versus whole-unit assembly scope, product generation, direction and reporting period. Weekly sales retain their measurement scope and period; a supplier denial is not an affirmative order reduction.

A coordinated release or disclosure is one action when its sources establish one coordinated rollout or disclosure batch with compatible versions. Keep its constituent capabilities or products together with their individual conditions; ordinary compatibility does not establish co-development. Distinguish physical design disclosures, system-code registry discoveries, independently developed products and customer shipment stages. Historical product roadmaps cannot become child events merely because the current article mentions their devices. Mixed sources must retain self-contained current system behavior and attributed organization assertions in the review queue when they are absent from the display shortlist. Review each local assertion separately from nearby commentary; neither a review flag nor the parent’s editorial tone decides its eligibility. Read explicit editorial recap/forecast framing from captured source units as well as selected facts, while preserving a current disclosure following historical context. Content-release projections require a source-local content-service context, not a service name in an unrelated OS article.


Resolve remedies by the operation that is actually asserted, not by every verb or by the shared incident alone. A carrier's customer notice, a released carrier-settings package, a newly imposed setup/update requirement, and Apple's replacement remedy may be independent actions when the source explicitly reports each. Advice to install an existing version is not another release or mandate. Bind each action to its affected device and network; classify software/settings and setup requirements as software, and physical device faults/replacements as hardware. Keep prevention, inability to restore already affected devices, and other operational limits with the relevant remedy. Background notices may complete a retained action without generating duplicate bullets.

An affirmative Apple commitment to provide replacement devices is replacement-remedy evidence too; it need not say that replacement is mandatory. For the same affected device and network, keep offered and required replacement coverage together, with any fee and eligibility differences attributed. A carrier's own offer, a denial, a tentative proposal or a historical program cannot establish Apple's current commitment. Earlier user concerns mentioned after a current announcement do not make that announcement historical.

Named OS permissions and first-party application surfaces define the changed object before the host hardware or third-party examples. Merge duplicate coverage of the same permission plan; keep different surfaces, versions, and planned versus shipped actions distinct. A newly disclosed bypass of a platform protection remains security news even when the underlying demonstration is older; retain the demonstration date, attribution and unknown applicability rather than treating those limits as automatic exclusion.


Resolve a complete title-led subject and predicate before generating identities from supporting paragraphs. A supplier's response about an Apple partnership is not its earnings result or a new procurement deal; a long-term component supply agreement is not ordinary price negotiation. A platform's awards result must retain its ceremony, result type and platform total, not inherit a program's rights-acquisition identity or individual prize count. A quantified production forecast may join translated coverage only when its reporting institution, product, fiscal quarter/year and production amount belong to the same forecast assertion; distinguish a total from a year-over-year reduction and a historical actual. Currency and unit-bearing quantities are not calendar years. Participation negation, including contractions, belongs to the named participant rather than a successor mentioned in the background.

For current code-discovery reports, require first-party code ownership, a shared attributed disclosure, platform version and primary component before treating feature lists as one bundle. Explicitly different beta builds are mandatory separation evidence even when an aggregate claim key matches. Do not merge unrelated releases, different component disclosures, or another discoverer's work merely because they share a beta version. Preserve the entire bundle's concrete features and all contributing source URLs. First-party browser/developer proposal calls remain software news even when partner names occur in their background; use the same Apple/WebKit action evidence for admission and relevance, and bind project names/years to the call's object rather than its publication month.

Current external-platform interoperability can be `ecosystem` when a named platform version introduces concrete cross-device capabilities for Apple endpoints, such as notifications, calls, file transfer or battery information. Ordinary App Store listing, accessory compatibility, historical support or unsupported future capability is insufficient. Keep the external platform as the action owner. Likewise, an industry observer listing Apple among potential infrastructure customers is not an Apple investment commitment without a specific reported transaction or project. When a secondary report adds attribution absent from its cited original, verify the claimed new evidence before overriding an editorial/old-report exclusion; do not infer a new disclosure from the secondary headline alone.

Group sources only when their own evidence positively identifies the same canonical subject and concrete action. The title and first substantive opening action define the primary subject; a local current assertion, captured key fact, or concrete attributed quotation may supply a missing relation or qualifier when it belongs to that same subject and action. Later unrelated background and a pooled bag of body terms cannot redefine it. A personal preference or biographical affiliation only modifies the actual subject; an executive liking a third-party brand does not make that brand’s action an Apple action. Conversely, promotional phrasing does not erase a locally attributed supply report about the title’s own device; retailer stock offers and explicitly recalled older constraints remain distinct. The absence of a conflict, a preliminary grouping, or transitive similarity is insufficient. A sparse source may join a more explicit source when a matching concrete claim supports that relationship and no incompatible subject, action, version, region, or stage is stated. Preserve all source URLs and their own facts when splitting mixed actions. Missing identity fields may remain unresolved; they must not bridge unrelated works, product lifecycles, facilities, legal stages, security notices, or hardware reports. The same pairing authority applies before output assembly and to compatibility entry points; summaries and source counts do not add independent merge permission.

Keep operational, disclosure, and procurement predicates authoritative over body background. A headline's primary subject/action owns event identity; a product roadmap does not become a CEO-transition event merely because its lead or later facts use an executive change as timing context. Reports about Apple retail-store layout or merchandising changes form one retail operation even when their bodies recap several future home products; do not project those background roadmaps into separate events. Cross-language reports of external testers evaluating the same unreleased Apple product may merge when the product and evaluation action match. For OS release trains, derive the primary channel and wave from the title-led release action; a public beta does not become the preceding developer beta merely because its lead mentions that earlier build. A beta, RC, or system build that accidentally exposes unannounced product assets is a software/system disclosure event even when the leaked asset is hardware, but a later explanatory article that says the assets were already reported and provides no new disclosure remains `weak`. Current attributed reports about the same Apple event schedule may merge even when sources name different subsets of the expected product lineup; product lists are supporting facts, not separate schedule identities. Bind the date to the source-owned launch clause, preserving confirmation versus reported plans; a reporter byline does not undo an explicit confirmation. OS releases, cancellations, delays, and negated launches retain their own subjects and stages, and a later background event cannot replace them. Reports about the same named Apple Wallet transit card becoming available may merge across headline angles when the card and availability action match. Apple component procurement is hardware/product news, but policy approval or blocking, supplier negotiation, order allocation, and completed procurement are different actions and must not merge merely because they name the same supplier or component. Shared component, year, product family, comparison wording, or source attribution is supporting evidence only and must not override an incompatible concrete subject/action; a non-Apple product that only compares itself with Apple remains `weak`.

For a source that names several products, generations, or works, project separate event variants only when the article explicitly assigns each one its own substantive action or exclusive fact; split every source fact into substantive sentences first, ignore products named only in `besides` / `除...外` background clauses, and retain only facts owned by each projected child. A calendar year, model year, anniversary, historical product list, comparison product, retirement timeline, already-sold product identifier, or confirmed existing regional model is never a product count or new action and must not create synthetic roadmap variants. Product-obsolescence lists, current hardware roadmaps, third-party software compatibility fixes, and first-party facility openings remain separate event types even when one article supplies historical context for another. A third-party vendor's customized, modified, luxury, or limited Apple-device edition remains `weak` unless Apple itself announces or changes the underlying product, price, availability, or policy.

A single video or report that discloses physical mockups, dummy units, or non-functional models for several unreleased Apple products is one disclosure action unless the source explicitly attributes separate reports or independent actions to those products. Preserve each model's distinct physical details in the same event; do not synthesize product-roadmap children merely because the page describes each mockup in a separate paragraph. This does not turn ordinary hands-on reviews of already released products into news.

Routine third-party accessories or apps that merely advertise compatibility with HomeKit, CarPlay, iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, or another existing Apple surface remain `weak` unless the title or lead establishes a new Apple platform, policy, approval, interoperability, data-access, procurement, or first-party product action. Conversely, a current attributed statement by an Apple executive about an Apple service, an official Apple product-lifecycle change, an Apple manufacturing or training facility action, or a first-party hardware/design patent is a direct Apple event and must not be demoted by incidental third-party context.

When cross-source titles take different editorial angles, a rare named subject can establish event identity only together with a compatible concrete action and Apple product/platform scope. Examples of rare subjects include a named first-party tool or project, a quoted feature name, a chip variant, or an internal model/codename. Use title and short-lead evidence first; key facts may contribute only those rare names or identifiers, not broad body background. Merge a pilot, cancellation, legal action, or product project across sources when the named subject and action match, even if one report emphasizes employee reaction or technical implementation. Keep different named tools/projects separate even when they share Genius Bar, Mac, AI, roadmap, testing, or publication timing.

Read quoted names as paired delimiters, preserving internal possessives and mixed-case brand names; an apostrophe in ordinary prose, an award label, a creator's earlier work, or a background acronym is not the current subject. For service content, bind the work to the title-owned action before reading catalog or release-date background. Where translated feature names differ, matching version, devices, shared resource, and concrete operation can establish the same capability, but shared platform/version alone cannot. Indirect hardware-disclosure headlines require a compatible model, changed property, and disclosure context; coincidentally matching counts are not enough. Bind a product launch and its year to the same primary assertion: a background Apple launch and another vendor's adoption date must never combine into an Apple roadmap or promote that vendor's story. Likewise, a corporate account's follow-list change is distinct from an executive opening a personal account, and a current application-distribution policy is not a new announcement of an older translation-layer support timeline.

A shared article, newsletter issue, informant, publication date or upcoming presentation is common provenance, not proof of one substantive operation. A broad report can supply source-owned facts to several actions without joining those actions; same-source coverage follows the same subject, operation and stage boundaries as cross-source coverage. A shared legal topic does not identify a case; require evidence of the same case and compatible procedural action.

Use crawler event groups as the starting point, then merge or split by the same standard used for the final brief. Treat each eligible JSON `event.id` as a hard boundary by default: one event becomes one bullet, and separate events must not be recombined with transition phrases such as `另有`, `同时`, `同一轮更新`, or `同属` merely to shorten the brief. Recombine two IDs only when both IDs' source titles and leads resolve to the same canonical subject and the same concrete action, and neither ID contains a distinct action or event-level fact, subject to the explicit claim/document lifecycle exceptions below. Match the independently focused action and stage, not an umbrella description such as a product update, roadmap or sales schedule. A price forecast, configuration disclosure, launch, pre-order opening and shipment remain separate when they are each a report's primary action; repeated prices, specifications or dates do not erase that boundary. Such details may still belong together within one established announcement or operation, so their mere presence is not a reason to split an existing event.

During deterministic reconciliation, preserve an already accepted crawler group unless title/lead evidence establishes an explicit incompatible action boundary. Exact canonical action signatures may reunite sparse and detailed cross-source reports, but generic token similarity may not. Derive event region, named app, product generation, legal case, and action stage from the title and opening lead before using body context: background countries, comparison products, source suffixes, and related-case details must neither split a duplicate nor bridge unrelated reports. For one legal case or platform-enforcement episode, keep a public response, injunction request, ruling, settlement, initial removal, and substantive follow-up as separate actions when the titles distinguish those stages; merge sources only within the matching stage. Explicit third-party accessories, unsupported tools, compatibility launches, competitor benchmarks, event countdowns, and product previews remain `weak` even if body noise creates an Apple-looking facet, while one verified current Apple action such as an OS release wave, official promotion, regional availability change, or certified-refurbished listing must not be downgraded merely because a headline uses roundup or deal-style wording.

Build reconciliation identity from four typed projections: concrete subject, concrete action/predicate, Apple product or platform scope, and independently extracted evidence such as a named report, period, percentage, amount, model, component, legal party, or action stage. Evidence is never a topic by itself, and one shared projection is insufficient when another non-empty projection conflicts. English aliases must match lexical boundaries and normal inflections rather than arbitrary substrings; CJK phrases may use contiguous matching. A weak editorial form such as an opinion, buying guide, deal, roundup, tutorial, podcast, or third-party spotlight must not bridge into a strong event even when it repeats the same product or metric; a multi-product event countdown or preview derived from existing rumors remains one weak roundup rather than spawning product events. Treat interoperability as `ecosystem` when a platform owner, operating-system component, hardware driver, or newly implemented system pairing and activation flow changes cross-platform behavior. An experimental implementation can qualify with its stated device and stage limits; a proposed or historical implementation, ordinary companion app, or utility that merely forwards data through an existing connection remains `weak`. Classify the implemented system operation as software, retaining its developer and supported-platform scope. A supplier-led headline remains direct Apple hardware news when its title identifies an Apple product, a concrete component, and a supplier qualification, procurement, or production relationship; do not downgrade it merely because the supplier is the grammatical subject. A claim and its direct denial, cancellation, restoration, or correction may reconcile only when the same typed subject and supporting evidence identify one claim lifecycle. For OS releases, the same platform/version/channel or one unambiguous cross-platform release wave may reconcile sparse titles, while a cross-source multi-feature roundup and a concrete feature discovery use separate identities; the latter merges only with reports of the same changed object.

Internal recall-oriented candidate groups are proposals for the reconciler; this does not cancel the default boundary of final JSON `events`. Before the reconciler accepts an article pair, apply explicit third-party ownership and concrete subject/action conflicts ahead of body-derived shared keys: an accessory, app, game, plugin, or utility remains a separate `weak` event when Apple is only its compatibility target or runtime. For two different URLs from the same publisher, require a shared structured action identity or matching concrete title components; generic service, product, platform, or background terms are insufficient. Keep a supplier's measured shipment/operating result separate from another supplier's hiring or factory-ramp action. Reconcile measured market reports by research firm, region, reporting period, metric, and Apple value; reports for different regions remain separate even when they share the firm, quarter, brands, or percentages. Product or service anniversaries are recap content only when the title reports a current milestone such as `turns seven` or `celebrates its anniversary`; a future anniversary-edition product name is a roadmap subject, not a retrospective.

A release announcement retains its primary release action; a recognized feature-list item must not replace it. Counts of upgrades describe properties, not counts of independently reported products. Bind quota changes to the counted object and scope: mail aliases, custom-domain addresses, and numbers of domains are different objects, even in one source. The same URL may support multiple separately bound actions. Compatible device names mentioned in an accessory's body cannot establish a shared action with a hardware roadmap. Code-discovered system capabilities keep their software object and version rather than inheriting the future host device's category.

Apple-owned recruitment is a current company action, including support and facilities roles. Keep the role or program as the event subject; a historical job posting or another employer's use of Apple equipment is not Apple's recruitment. A new operating-system patcher, driver or system adaptation that enables OS compatibility or hardware acceleration on Apple hardware qualifies as ecosystem news, including experimental or virtualized support. The report must establish newly implemented system or graphics adaptation, not merely an existing virtual machine’s configuration or an app running on Mac. Native installation and official endorsement are not prerequisites; retain the stated hardware, functional and security limits. An independent utility using existing configuration and resource-management facilities remains weak.

Project a detail page into child events before reconciliation only when the page independently states multiple named first-party subjects with their own concrete actions and locally supporting facts; each child keeps the source URL but receives its own subject, action, summary, and facts. A list of related titles, background works, products, or services without local action evidence must remain context rather than create synthetic children. A platform, component or capability described only as part of a device disclosure remains a supporting fact unless the source establishes a separately focused action; a distinct name, subsection heading or software/hardware category does not supply that evidence. Resolve relevance and category once from each article or projected claim's title-led owner, subject, action, and changed object; an aggregated event summary, body-only background, or another member's Apple terms must not later promote, demote, reclassify, or bridge that decision.

When titles or opening leads expose a concrete fact signature, reconcile on the combined subject and changed object rather than on the product name alone. Examples of reusable changed objects include an exact finish set, case material, form-factor redesign, program operation, named future generation, or first-party document lifecycle. Compatible exact signatures may join reports across languages; incompatible explicit signatures keep independent actions separate, while later background alternatives must not replace the first concrete title/lead fact. App Store removal stages require an explicit App Store/application-store surface, not merely the words Apple plus removed or deleted. A crime blotter/roundup, a future-record projection explicitly derived from existing rumors without new reporting, or a non-Apple project using Apple Park or an Apple product only as a size/performance benchmark remains `weak`.

Merge articles or final bullets only after source review shows duplicate coverage of the same core subject and action: the same product, app, service, content title, ranking/chart, playlist, subscription offer, component, feature, policy, legal/company move, hardware item, region rollout, or official announcement. Shared Apple context, OS version, WWDC timing, product family, Apple Intelligence theme, source timing, broad event kind, generic Apple TV/Apple Music/Apple One service context, or generic terms such as Apple, App Store, developer, legal, regulation, Vision Pro, or AI are never enough. The same program or product lineage, an upstream/downstream or benefit/risk relationship, a launch followed by enforcement or restriction, and a source URL projected into multiple events are also not duplicate proof; preserve separate IDs when the actions differ. One narrow exception is the lifecycle of the exact same first-party page, document, or configuration: an initial appearance/publication and a direct follow-up reporting its removal, withdrawal, correction, restoration, or replacement form one chronological event, with the initial facts, changed status, and all source URLs preserved. Do not use this exception to merge a later product launch, approval, enforcement action, policy decision, or independently sourced development. Service/content events with different content titles or actions, such as a trailer/special, sports schedule, event lineup, awards result, season renewal, playlist, or chart, must remain separate bullets even when they share Apple TV, Apple Music, or the same publication window. Apple service payment-method or billing-availability stories merge only when the region, platform/service, payment method, and restore/compliance action are the same; do not merge them with unrelated iCloud+ entitlement, OS beta, Wallet, Apple Pay/Card promotion, or App Store policy stories merely because they share App Store, iCloud, payment, subscription, or India context.

For an official Apple launch-event campaign, derive identity only from a title-owned event schedule, an official campaign name/tagline, or an official campaign asset such as an event mark or Hashmoji; event references found only in production, survey, attendance, rumor, or background copy must not pull those independent actions into the campaign. Reconcile localized campaign coverage transitively when the campaign name and the same occurrence agree, including adjacent calendar dates caused by time-zone conversion, but keep title-owned attendee activities and production reports separate. An exact shared primary action may outweigh conflicting secondary background facets, while independently titled launch, price, display, order, or production actions remain separate. A measured market-result duplicate must agree on the reporting firm, Apple product, region, period, metric, and observed-result action; a sparse translated source may join through a corroborated exact relation, but generic percentages, rankings, forecasts, or product mentions are insufficient. Editorial product comparisons, guessed dates, concept renders, multi-vendor price summaries, and competitor-led comparisons remain `weak`; a current attributed filing, leak, report, or first-party capability such as an Apple Support AI rollout or Genlock support remains eligible. If a selected detail page fails transiently, one bounded retry is allowed before recording a terminal failure; do not broaden discovery, loop indefinitely, or silently substitute unrelated listing text.

For one service price-change action, normalize equivalent price language across sources and merge duplicate coverage, but preserve every distinct affected-country list, percentage/range, localized currency or plan example, effective date, and explicitly unchanged region in `key_facts` or `must_include_facts`. Do not apply hardware-price compression to a service-price event merely because an article contains generic cost or price background. OS public-beta release or hands-on coverage may merge only with the same platform/version/channel action; a regional Apple Intelligence filing, approval, or availability action remains separate even when both articles mention the same OS release.

When title wording differs across publishers, an exact same-action identity may still merge after concrete subject and action checks pass; source suffixes, generic executive labels such as `CEO`, incidental regions in body background, and broad Apple/product words must neither split that duplicate nor bridge unrelated events. Apple-specific supplier commercial outlook or component-revenue exposure is a distinct supply-chain action: merge reports only when they share the same supplier and Apple business/component outlook, and keep them separate from Apple market share, device sales, supplier procurement, or restricted-supplier negotiations.

Cross-language executive-tenure retrospectives may merge only when the title or opening lead identifies the same named Apple executive, the same tenure length or anniversary, and the same retrospective action. Keep different executives, different tenure lengths, current appointments or strategy statements, and unrelated company milestones separate; product, service, financial, or historical lists inside the retrospective are supporting facts and must not split the duplicate report or bridge it to current product events.

Reports of Apple legal proceedings may merge only when they identify the same parties, case or alleged conduct, and concrete procedural action. A request for Apple to explain is the sender's inquiry, not Apple's reply; keep the sender, application, planned or unconfirmed stage, and any explicit date of that letter bound to its own source. Distinct inquiries and replies remain separate even when they recount the same historical app removal. Dates of another participant's background letter cannot define the current action, and relative dates do not supply an absolute year. Concrete case signatures such as the same named app or service, plaintiff count, claimed-loss amount, filing venue, or alleged conduct may establish cross-language identity when headlines use different editorial angles. Case identity takes precedence over unrelated product, prototype, acquisition, personnel, battery, board, or software details included as litigation background; those body details must not split one case into multiple events. A first-party product design patent being granted, published, or explored is a hardware/design disclosure, not a patent lawsuit: merge it only with coverage of the same patented product/design, and never with an infringement verdict merely because both mention `patent`. Separate genuinely new lawsuits, rulings, appeals, settlements, or enforcement actions even when they involve the same company. Commentary that only says a person may, might, or could be subpoenaed, testify, become involved, or be drawn into discovery remains `weak` until a subpoena, filing, ruling, testimony, confirmation, or other concrete procedural action actually occurs; it must not merge into a current filing or response merely because it discusses the same lawsuit.

Normalize equivalent legal stages across languages before reconciling the same case. In particular, a court reviving, reinstating, restoring, reopening, or allowing a previously dismissed case to proceed describes one case-revival action when the parties and alleged conduct match. Independent-repair litigation should use the repair restriction or access claim as the case subject; generic Apple, court, lawsuit, or hardware terms are insufficient, and a later ruling, settlement, appeal, or new filing remains a separate procedural action.

Read the complete headline before assigning its dominant action. A background or transition clause at the start of a headline must not override a later concrete document, product-policy response, legal filing stage, social-account action, or other title-owned change. Reports about the same executive's formal appointment, compensation disclosure, first employee memo, social-account launch, profile-image change, and departure from a separate role are distinct actions unless the sources explicitly cover the same action. Conversely, reports quoting product plans from the same first employee memo belong to that document event when the title or opening lead attributes the plans to the memo; do not project the quoted plans into independent supply-chain events. Different procedural stages in the same legal case remain separate even when their bodies repeat the same case history. In a joint filing, each party owns its requests and responses; a named party inside another speaker's allegation does not become the speaker. Keep conditionally scheduled follow-up procedures with the request they qualify. Current leadership interviews may explain earlier transitions; establish the same interview instance and speaker before grouping complementary coverage, and distinguish resurfaced old interviews. Corporate reporting-line changes and independently appointed service leaders retain their own functional categories; names of services in a corporate biography do not determine the company event's category. Praise, criticism, or retrospective judgment from an outside investor, analyst, or commentator remains `weak` unless it also reports a current Apple action or a concrete measured market result.

Split a group before writing when it has `merge_warnings`, mixed event kinds or relevance tiers, incompatible regions, multiple primary subjects, multiple concrete actions, or a broad recap article bridging specific reports. Use source titles and `key_facts` to assign facts to separate events. Non-actionable weekly recap pages such as `Top Stories` should not be treated as a source for a separate specific hardware/software report when the standalone report exists.

Treat the detail-page title and its first substantive sentence as the primary event identity, resolved as a concrete subject/product, component or counterparty, action, and scope. The title controls; the lead may fill a missing identity anchor but must not replace a concrete title subject or action. A shared broad product, platform, service, source attribution, date, or topic facet never overrides incompatible non-empty subject/action assertions; conversely, matching normalized subject and action assertions may reconcile sparse cross-language reports when neither side states a conflicting assertion. Once a detail page supplies a substantive summary or concrete facts, listing/feed excerpts must not be appended to its identity or merge tokens; use discovery text only when the detail parse is genuinely sparse. Product/platform enumerations, historical context, comparisons, related cases, cast lists, recommended links, source-name suffixes, and domain labels such as `COM` may provide context, but they must not create a second identity or bridge unrelated events. Facts extracted from related/recommended links must not promote a non-Apple title subject into a direct Apple procurement, product, or platform action. In particular, a non-Apple title subject remains `weak` when Apple appears only in a body list, affected-platform list, or comparison, and it must not merge with a direct Apple event merely because both receive the same broad topic facet; this includes a third-party app, bot, or vulnerability whose own action happens to affect iPhone, Mac, iOS, or macOS. A company name is a legal identity anchor only when the title or lead also states an actual filing, ruling, settlement, demand, or other legal action; a background mention of the same company is insufficient. Likewise, `supply chain sources` is attribution, not a display-panel supply-chain action unless the report also identifies a supplier, order, production, shipment, or supply change. Price coverage must distinguish a current price change from a forecast, promotion, refurbished listing, supplier dispute, market reaction, or outside commentary even when all articles repeat the same earlier increase. A concrete Apple market result measured by smartphone revenue, revenue share, shipments, or market share remains a market-performance event even when a headline says competitor price increases contributed to the result; keep it separate from a future Apple-product price forecast. Likewise, a broad multi-product roadmap must not absorb a specific delay, price, component, or production subevent found in its body. Two `weak` or deferred articles may merge only when they share an explicit title-level event identity or sufficient non-generic title-level subject and action anchors; an Apple/iPhone comparison, related-page fragment, compatibility list, or generic brand word found only in summaries or body facts must not bridge them. Direct-Apple polls, podcasts, analyses, and non-actionable roundups may remain discoverable in `deferred_events`, but they do not become required brief items unless they contain a separately verified first-party platform or product change. In particular, a publisher-reader poll about purchase intent or device upgrade cycles remains `weak` even when a later paragraph cites an analyst; `upgrade` in that context is not an analyst rating action. Listing Apple TV, CarPlay, iPad, Mac, or Apple silicon as rollout coverage does not by itself turn a Siri/Apple Intelligence availability report into separate content, vehicle-platform, tablet-chip, or hardware-roadmap events.

Use typed claim identity when ordinary title tokens differ across languages. A hardware repair-cost report may merge only on the same canonical Apple product, repaired component, repair-cost action, and normalized amount; whole-device purchase prices and competitor comparison prices are supporting facts, not the repair amount. Third-party Apple-platform compatibility reports may merge only on the same named project, Apple platform or chip generation, and compatible release/development milestone. Unreleased first-party hardware roadmap reports may merge only on one canonical product, a future launch/reveal/ship action, and the same future year; a multi-product lineup, event-preview roundup, anniversary retrospective, or body-only historical date must never supply that roadmap identity. A versioned Apple OS or CarPlay name is context rather than action ownership when the title's current action is only a third-party app or service becoming available; promote it only when Apple or the platform itself changes an API, framework, policy, interoperability rule, approval, or first-party capability.

Resolve action ownership from the title together with the first substantive lead, not from grammatical word order alone. A headline that leads with CarPlay, iPhone, Mac, Apple Watch, or another Apple platform still remains a third-party app action when the lead identifies an outside app or vendor as the party launching or updating the capability; keep it `weak` unless the report also establishes a separate Apple API, framework, policy, approval, interoperability, security, or first-party feature change. Conversely, differently ordered or translated coverage of the same first-party product experiment may merge when it shares the canonical Apple product, the same accessory or component, and the same concrete tested, considered, adopted, rejected, or launch action. Prototype support that was evaluated and later omitted is one compatibility-evaluation event, not separate positive and negative events.

Treat facilities, product capabilities, and organization changes as separate typed subjects even when one article supplies background for another. A partner or Apple facility opening, expansion, or investment merges only on the same facility type, location, counterparty, and facility action; innovation, research, engineering, education, developer, and manufacturing facilities must not be absorbed into a product-feature or product-roadmap event merely because the facility will supply that product. Executive succession or management changes likewise must not merge with product roadmaps through shared Apple, CEO, design, future-product, or publication context.

For a genuine title-led multi-product hardware report, split the source into product-scoped claims before reconciliation when each product has its own component, availability, timing, or roadmap action. Coordinating phrases such as `alongside`, `together with`, or their cross-language equivalents mark a new product/action clause only when locally supported facts follow; attach the shared source URL to each valid child without using the parent article as a bridge. A product/component adoption claim may then merge across languages only when the canonical product, physical component, and adoption or launch action agree. Do not infer this identity from dynamic price, capacity, version, or comparison values, and do not project body-only products from an ordinary single-subject report.

For manual fallback reconciliation, identify the concrete action before comparing general topic similarity. Apple-operated Trade In valuation changes are first-party retail/product-policy events rather than ordinary retailer deals; merge multilingual coverage when the valuation action is the same and retain each source's model-level values. A current Apple market-share result remains direct Apple market news even when the report names competitors. In a vendor-neutral device-market report, locally attributed Apple measurements in the body or table can establish relevance; merely listing the Apple name cannot. Treat a third-party model or app that merely runs on iPhone, iPad, Mac, or iOS as `weak`; later runtime, accessory, comparison, or related-link facts must not override a direct first-party title/lead action in the opposite direction. For legal coverage, distinguish filing, dismissal request, public response, schedule/discovery change, ruling, injunction, and settlement, and merge only matching parties, case subject, and action stage. A report about evidence disclosure may describe the same current joint status filing across languages; bind the filing in its current source context. A historical joint report does not turn later disclosure completion, postponement, a ruling or an appeal into that filing, and shared litigants alone cannot bridge these actions. For supply-chain coverage, keep supplier qualification, capacity constraints, procurement or price negotiation, component stockpiling, device bill-of-materials forecasts, product supply constraints, government supplier-approval policy, component-production bottlenecks, and broad industry pricing as separate actions even when all mention the same memory vendor, display technology, product family, or chip generation.

Determine the action owner before applying Apple product, platform, version, price, or feature anchors. A third party launching, updating, pricing, comparing, or making a product compatible with an Apple platform remains `weak` when Apple is only the runtime, recipient platform, compatibility target, benchmark, or speculative comparison; this includes a third-party device whose own sale or launch headline merely says it supports Apple Home, HomeKit, or Apple devices. Promote it only when the report establishes a separate direct Apple platform, interoperability, data, App Store enforcement, security, legal, procurement, or first-party product action. First-person experience, frustration, upgrade-motivation, and multi-year rumor-catalog articles remain editorial or roundup items unless their title and opening lead independently establish an eligible action or Apple-device measurement. Retain a source-owned quantitative test and its conditions even within an otherwise personal review; specifications, impressions and citations to earlier reports do not establish such a test. For duplicate detection across languages, compare typed identities such as the person and organizational role in a personnel action, the service and entitlement in a benefit change, the platform component and feature action in an OS report, the named work and production/content action in entertainment coverage, or the parties and procedural stage in a legal case. Do not invent a subject from an action phrase, and do not merge on shared Apple terms, OS versions, release dates, episode counts, publication metadata, or generic verbs. Reports about different built-in apps, watch faces, service titles, or legal stages remain separate even when they share the same platform or date.

When a single article expressly reports multiple Apple products, generations, or service titles, do not preserve it as one event merely because it has one URL. Project separate variants only when the title or first substantive lead establishes a genuine aggregate roadmap, lineup, or catalog report and each subject has its own substantive, exclusive fact or action; merely naming several models in a price, finish, production, or other focused report is not sufficient. Apply that structural projection consistently across languages: a translated aggregate report must not remain as a parent event alongside product-scoped children from another source. Within a labelled product section, the section heading owns its continuation facts; a comparison, compatibility reference, or borrowed design name in a later sentence must not redirect those facts to another product. Projected siblings remain separate canonical products, and a generic child must not absorb a distinct project cancellation, retention, delay, or other explicit claim-status report. When that aggregate roadmap assigns distinct claims to explicit generations of the same product family, preserve those generations as separate child boundaries so later-generation facts cannot disappear into the current model. Retain the shared source URL on every resulting event, make the projected product/action authoritative over aggregate parent-page background, and keep common facts out of all variant identities. A focused finish-lineup disclosure remains separate from a broad product roadmap unless both sources explicitly report the same typed finish action and compatible values. Ordinary OS build numbers, security text, compatibility lists, body-only comparison products, and later background paragraphs must not trigger hardware projection or identity bridges. Do not split an ordinary single-subject report merely because its body mentions comparison products or adjacent roadmap context.

When one article could match several existing groups, assign it to the group with the strongest concrete title/lead identity: a shared first-party app or program name, named component, supplier/counterparty, product scope, and compatible action outrank generic token overlap or whichever event happened to be created first. A bridge article that explicitly reports multiple independent numbered announcements or title-level actions must contribute separately scoped facts to those events rather than join them into one cluster; body background alone must not create another event, and event-to-event consolidation needs direct pairwise support across both groups. Apply the same rule to broad product roadmaps: project only facts explicitly tied to each named product/model and concrete action, and never use the broad source to bridge otherwise distinct products. Apple-owned apps, programs, leasing plans, patents, and official product communications remain direct Apple events even when a financing partner, supplier, or real-world user appears in the report. A device-leasing or upgrade program and an overdue-payment restriction are separate actions even when one article reports both, so each may receive only its own facts and matching sources. Conversely, a third-party device, service, poll, or competitor comparison remains `weak` when Apple, AirPlay, HomeKit, Apple Music, or an Apple product appears only as compatibility, comparison, hypothetical, follow-up, or imitation context; a title saying that Sony, Microsoft, Google, Samsung, or another company follows or imitates an Apple program still reports the third party's action, not a new Apple action.

Product-led paragraphs and explicit section headings retain their local design and capability facts, including possessive product names such as Apple’s glasses. Keep generation and display/audio/camera conditions with that product; stop inherited ownership at a different product.

Reports of the same platform operation can share an event when supported device models are applicability conditions rather than separate launches or activation stages. Preserve each model and host requirement; ordinary media playback, default system output, and television surround sound remain different operations.

For a genuine multi-product first-party hardware roadmap, project one child event per explicit product family and action, then merge cross-source child events only when their Apple product identity and roadmap/availability action are compatible. Different variants or dimensions within the same named product family may remain facts in that child event when an umbrella source explicitly reports the family roadmap; they must not bridge that family to Apple TV, HomePod, another hardware line, or an executive-strategy story. A shared first-party program name likewise outranks incidental price, device, partner, or regional facets when every source reports the same launch or plan change. Direct Apple product roadmaps, AppleCare plan changes, Apple security bulletins that credit outside researchers, and attributed Apple executive strategy or succession statements remain direct Apple events even when third-party companies, AI models, partners, competitors, or historical comparisons dominate parts of the body.

Treat product-configuration numbers such as screen sizes, memory capacities, model counts, and prices as facts, never as numbered first-party announcements. Reports of the same model's dimension/battery configuration keep their attributed figures together, while another model, actual launch, or a denial remains distinct. Different informants or complementary parameters do not by themselves establish separate product actions; compatible coverage does not require a jointly issued report. A host phone cannot own a home-platform setup feature, and a reporting date cannot turn a specification disclosure into a launch schedule. Keep an Apple-operated certified-refurbished listing separate from a future hardware roadmap and from an unrelated employer's disposal of Apple devices; the latter remains `weak` unless Apple itself changes a product, service, policy, or supply action. Editorial questions, wish-list wording, or commentary framing must not demote a concrete current versioned first-party OS feature, built-in-app removal, material App Store enforcement/restoration action, or Apple-maintained compatibility/certification-list update, while a reader purchase poll or a third-party developer tool update that merely runs on an Apple platform remains `weak`. Distinguish first-party feature objects even when they share one app: for example, a Wallet account-card regional expansion and physical-pass import are separate actions. Merge cross-language product-anniversary coverage only when the product and milestone action match. When a title or lead explicitly covers several future Apple product families or upgrade options, project product-specific roadmap events and attach only each product's own facts, so a broad source can support multiple correct events without bridging them together; assign an `Ultra`, foldable, Pro, Air, or other subtype label only when that child's fact scope is exclusive to that subtype, never when the same projected fact group also contains another base model or variant. Generic release wording in an OS, security, jailbreak, or compatibility report is not a hardware-roadmap signal.

Editorial framing does not by itself weaken a current first-party action when the title and opening lead independently state that Apple has launched, listed, made available, or materially repriced its own product or accessory; such a hands-on or review source may merge with direct launch coverage when the product and action agree. By contrast, a third-party resale-value study, trade-in estimate, shopping alternative, or imperative recommendation to replace an Apple upgrade with another product remains `weak` unless Apple itself changed a trade-in value, retail offer, product, or policy.

Apple event scheduling and invitation-art interpretation are related but distinct actions. Merge invitation/logo interpretations only when they concern the same event, Apple product family, and compatible feature or design clues; keep them separate from Apple's official date, venue, streaming, or occurrence announcement. Likewise, keep a current customer's pending-order substitution or free-upgrade action separate from the product generation roadmap, launch availability, and specification disclosures for that same hardware, even when all reports share a chip generation and publication window.

For cross-language service/content production coverage, merge only when the reports identify the same named work, compatible season, and the same concrete production action; a generic headline may take the missing work name from its first substantive sentence when that sentence explicitly ties the named work to a rights acquisition, order, renewal, release, or other concrete Apple TV action. Trailer, cast, platform, or generic Apple TV words alone are not identity. Disaster-relief coverage may merge on the same normalized affected region and Apple's donation or rebuilding action even when one headline names Tim Cook and another names Apple. Product pre-order schedule reports may merge on the same specific model and reported calendar date despite secondary-product context, but that shared schedule must not absorb a separately stated launch window for another model. A named Steve Jobs or Apple historical artifact auction is a direct Apple-history hardware event when the item and completed sale match across sources; surrounding auction lots remain supporting facts. Material Apple market-cap or share-move coverage may merge when the same ranking change, counterparty, percentage, valuation, or closing-price signature is present; unrelated historical price-change background must not split or bridge that market event.

Treat supplier input-cost changes and Apple device/service retail-price changes as separate identities. Cross-source supplier-cost coverage may merge when it shares the same named supplier or foundry, the same cost/price action, and a direct effect on Apple components or products. Do not merge it with a model-specific consumer price forecast merely because both mention a future iPhone price, and do not classify a broad industry price report as direct Apple news unless the title or lead states the concrete Apple cost or sourcing impact.

For Apple Messages for Business or iMessage AI-agent stories, merge cross-source coverage only when the stories share a specific entity such as Poke, or when they share the messaging-platform anchor plus AI-agent and approval/integration action anchors. Do not merge those stories with generic local AI apps or third-party tools just because they mention iPhone, Mac, AI, or Messages in passing.

For Vision Pro stories where Apple's headset is the core tool behind a third-party professional, enterprise, medical, training, theme-park, or spatial/immersive experience, preserve the concrete project or venue as the event subject. Merge cross-source coverage that shares the same project/entity, but do not merge it with unrelated Vision Pro rumors, Apple hardware sightings, Beats/headphone stories, or other items merely because they share Apple, Vision Pro, publication timing, or generic action words.

Preserve granular events during high-volume WWDC, OS-preview, and roadmap windows. Built-in apps, OS components, platform APIs, developer tools, service/content items, hardware rumors, regional rollouts, compatibility lists, feature lists, security/support-document changes, and third-party accessory compatibility stories stay separate when their concrete action differs. Built-in-app or component changes merge only when sources share the same OS platform family and concrete component/action; a generic `app`, OS version, or `built-in-app-change` label must not bridge iOS Messages, watchOS app removal, Mail, Weather, Notes, Shortcuts, or other independent changes. OS release items require the same normalized version train, release channel, stage, and action. A coordinated developer/public beta batch may span Apple platforms when those release anchors match, but final releases should remain platform-specific, with iOS/iPadOS companion coverage allowed to stay together; a cross-platform security bulletin is a separate security event rather than a bridge between platform release events. Build summary and key-fact scope from the title plus the first non-duplicate lead action, so another platform's final release, a different version's beta, or a separate security bulletin mentioned as body background cannot leak into the current event's facts. A detail-page lead may correct a stale numbered-beta listing headline, but a specific feature discovery, regional availability decision, tutorial, recap, or support-policy explanation remains its own event. Generic `security`, `vulnerability`, `fix`, or CVE language never establishes event identity by itself: security reports must share the same OS version/bulletin or the same concrete exploit, component, and attack path, and current releases must not absorb third-party app vulnerabilities or legacy-version patches. Exact regional actions such as approval, filing, launch, continued absence, or delayed availability must come from the title/lead and must not be inferred from background in a general beta experience article. Do not merge current-version betas, legacy-version RCs, public security releases, and support-document/security-policy explanations merely because they were published on the same day. For example, Mail search, Weather forecasts, Safari browser features, Messages drawing/Markup changes, Notes features, Shortcuts generation, Recovery mode, Apple Wallet/Digital ID identity verification, Xcode integration, Apple TV Remote, App Store subscriptions, Apple TV season renewals, Apple Music playlists, Apple One or credit-card offers, watchOS app removal, iOS input-method changes, CarPlay route-sharing APIs, Terminal paste protection, device color/mockup rumors, and Beats/headphone hardware sightings are separate topics unless the sources explicitly report one combined change. Siri AI / Apple Intelligence regulatory talks with EU or DMA officials should merge across policy, software, and leadership angles when the concrete action is the same Cook/EU meeting about launching Siri AI in Europe. App Store legal appeals such as Apple/Epic Supreme Court proceedings remain strong legal events and must not be downgraded just because the article body mentions Apple Pay, third-party payments, commissions, or fee percentages. Hardware roadmap items also stay split by concrete product and action: iPhone Air successor timing, foldable iPhone render leaks, iPhone Pro design rumors, future device testing lists, component changes, and tag/list pages must not merge solely because they share Apple, iPhone, future-year, or roadmap context.

When merging cross-source OS point-release internal-testing reports, require the same platform family, the same version, and the same internal-testing action. A current report that Apple is internally testing iOS 27.4 may merge across languages even if one source includes broad iOS 27.0 feature background, but it must not merge with macOS/watchOS/tvOS/visionOS internal-testing items that share only a version number. Apple Watch band or wearable-sensor rumors should merge across languages when the shared action is sensor integration into the band or wearable hardware, even if one source adds health, patent, blood-glucose, blood-pressure, or sweat-sensor background. Apple supplier data-leak coverage should merge across languages when it shares concrete anchors such as the supplier, dark-web disclosure, stolen/confidential files, regulator/security-team investigation, or leaked iPhone model files; it should not merge with separate sensor, battery, chip-process, price, or design leaks unless the source explicitly treats them as the same disclosure item.

Apple company organization, leadership, and design-team authority stories are separate from Apple product pricing, component-cost, memory-shortage, or supply-cost pressure stories. Merge cross-source reports within each subject when they share the same company-structure action or the same pricing/cost action, but do not merge the two merely because both mention Apple executives, Bloomberg/Gurman context, future products, or the same publication window.

Apple executive movement and organization changes require a concrete organization or personnel action such as leaving, joining, poaching, appointment, succession, management restructuring, design-team authority changes, or equivalent Chinese phrasing. Person names, CEO titles, Gurman/Bloomberg context, product-design language, or "industrial design" details inside a hardware roadmap story are not enough to convert a product event into a company-organization event.

Apple strategic transaction, merger, acquisition, or buyout stories merge across languages and sources only when they share the same transaction counterparty and concrete action. A report about Apple and Disney merger discussions should merge with another Apple/Disney merger-discussion report, but not with a separate Apple acquisition or partnership discussion involving a different company. When the title's lead action is Apple acquiring, merging with, buying, or filing for a transaction involving a named counterparty, keep it as a strong Apple company/developer-platform event even if the body contains App Store status, third-party app, or related-link background; weak third-party app/service items must not be absorbed into that transaction unless they share the same counterparty and action. Executive or leadership meetings with government officials are company/regional Apple events when the concrete subject is Apple's investment, jobs, regulation, or platform behavior; when the same meeting is specifically about Siri AI, DMA, or another named regulatory platform issue, keep it with that specific regulatory event family.

Developer-tool and Apple platform infrastructure stories can merge across languages when they clearly share the same project or platform entity and action, such as the same Swift package index, package-signing system, documentation host, TestFlight/Xcode capability, or open-source developer project joining Apple or changing Apple platform development. Generic shared words such as developer, package, Swift, open source, or Apple are not enough without the shared project/entity anchor.

Cross-source coverage of the same official education promotion may merge when country or region, campaign timing, eligibility, and offer action match, even when one headline leads with the purchased Mac or iPad and another leads with the included AirPods or Apple Pencil reward. Preserve every source's distinct eligibility, reward, price, and timing details.

Cross-language coverage of a first-party Apple education, training, developer, or manufacturing facility may merge when the facility type, country or region, and opening, establishment, or expansion action agree. An explicitly named first-party facility such as an Apple Education Center controls the subject over a later sentence that describes its country as a manufacturing center; background training/manufacturing terms must not change the facility type or bridge it to an unrelated donation, community-support, or staffing action. Training language alone is never Apple Intelligence evidence: do not infer an AI/model event from substrings inside ordinary education, training, manufacturing, or staffing terms.

OS signing-status items merge across languages only when the concrete action is Apple stopping, closing, or no longer signing the same iOS/iPadOS version family, preventing downgrade or rollback. Do not confuse this with package signing, code signing, identity signing, or unrelated developer/security signing systems. An OS release identity must read its version from a number directly attached to the named OS or software train; prices, prize amounts, code-line counts, dates, percentages, and other unrelated numbers in the article must never create an OS-version facet or bridge events. Use the internal-testing identity only when the article explicitly says Apple is internally testing the version or cites Apple engineer, visitor-log, access-log, or equivalent evidence; ordinary wording that developers are testing a released beta/RC must retain its beta/RC version and channel identity.

A sales forecast binds its reporting institution, full device model, locally stated volume, and forecast period before market context is considered. Different-language coverage of that same claim may merge; a later market-share percentage, another institution's total-market estimate, production constraint, or historical comparison cannot replace those anchors. Keep different institutions, models, periods, volumes, and current versus historical or denied assertions separate.

Hardware market and supply-chain reports must also stay split by concrete subject and action. Display-panel/OLED mass-production stories, broad multi-product panel allocation/orders, single-product foldable-iPhone panel production, foldable-iPhone supply timing or hinge-production stories, foldable-iPhone launch/preorder/delay/normal-delivery timing stories, second-generation foldable-iPhone or iPhone Ultra 2 project approval/greenlight stories, Apple design-team/company-organization changes, Apple product price/cost-pressure stories, Apple product/supplier data-leak stories, Apple silicon or Mac chip roadmap stories, iPhone/foldable roadmap rumors, and competitor product comparisons are separate event families unless the same source explicitly reports them as one Apple action for the same product scope. Same-source or cross-source foldable-iPhone production/order-target reports may merge when they share the same concrete Apple production target, order volume, build target, or supplier instruction, such as a 10-million-unit order/target, even if one article's body contains related foldable-panel, price, memory-shortage, or broader iPhone-production background; the title's lead production/order action should control the event boundary. Same-source or cross-source foldable-iPhone launch-timing reports may merge when the title/lead action itself is about the same foldable-iPhone launch, preorder, delay, or delivery-timing claim; do not merge competitor launch articles or unrelated iPhone Pro design/price stories merely because their body compares timing with Apple's foldable roadmap. Same-source or cross-source foldable-iPhone panel market reports may merge when they share the same panel/display shipment, order-share, supplier, Counterpoint-style market-share, or revenue-growth action; they must stay separate from foldable-iPhone production-target, model-count, launch-timing, hinge-production, price, or roadmap reports unless source review proves one combined Apple action. Apple-specific market-share or shipment reports merge only when they share the same product family, same reported metric, same reporting period, and compatible research/source framing; keep them separate from OS betas, service-entitlement stories, how-to guides, broad market roundups, and product-roadmap rumors even when they share Apple, AI, Counterpoint, growth, or shipment wording. For price-heavy days, split by concrete pricing action rather than the shared presence of memory, storage, cost, or price terms: official Apple product price increases and Apple explanations/responses, reseller or retailer attempts to retroactively change completed Apple-product orders, external executive or analyst reactions, analyst stock-target updates such as Apple/AAPL target-price changes tied to a named firm, supplier or component-cost pass-through disputes, Apple requests for government approval to buy memory/storage from a restricted or blacklisted supplier, official refurbished-store or retail availability, official retail/customer-acquisition promotions such as Back to School, future product price forecasts such as future iPhone / foldable iPhone pricing, Apple-specific market-share or shipment reports, and broad component-industry background stories are separate event families unless source review proves duplicate coverage of the same action. Future product price forecasts should not merge with foldable-screen, hinge, launch-timing, production, mockup, or supply-chain events merely because they concern the same unreleased Apple product family. MacBook or Mac product price, shipment, market-forecast, or component-cost stories are price/market events, not MacBook chip/product-roadmap events merely because their title contains `MacBook`, `2026`, `2027`, shipment forecasts, or pricing terms. Apple memory supplier sourcing involving CXMT/YMTC/长鑫/长江存储, restricted/blacklisted supplier approval, or China-market memory procurement may merge across languages when the subject is the same sourcing/approval action, but it must stay separate from iPhone production cuts, foldable panel orders, official price increases, reseller disputes, and general memory-shortage background. For product or supplier leak-heavy days, the title's lead action decides the primary event boundary: iPhone production/shipments/demand forecasts, foldable-iPhone production or panel-order targets, foldable-iPhone launch timing, iPhone Pro physical-dimension or camera-plateau thickness rumors, logic-board/chip/memory schematic leaks, camera hardware leaks, camera patents, DMCA/takedown or legal enforcement, schematics/data-sheet/specification disclosures, visual color/mockup/SIM-tray/drop-test leaks, foldable-iPhone or iPhone Ultra prototype stories, and official refurbished-store availability are separate event families even when the body reuses the same breach, Tata/supplier, dark-web, future-iPhone, camera, thickness, or production background. First-party Final Cut Camera or Creator Studio app updates are software/app events, not iPhone camera hardware leak or patent events, even when the feature is exclusive to a specific iPhone model. Weak or non-Apple-subject articles without a clear Apple action should not be absorbed into a stronger Apple hardware event just because they share generic words such as display, chip, price, Pro, production, supply, privacy, design, future-year model names, or leak/rumor framing; in particular, a generic product-rumor phrase such as `Leak claims...` is not the same as a product data breach involving schematics, data sheets, stolen files, supplier systems, or dark-web file leaks, and a background note that a first-generation foldable iPhone uses a supplier-provided display is not enough to turn a successor-roadmap story into a supply-chain event.

Within the broader memory-supply theme, Apple supplier testing/procurement/qualification, an Apple shipment-plan change, order backlog or capacity allocation, government or national-security restrictions, and general industry capacity are separate actions unless the titles and leads identify the same concrete development; shared supplier names, component class, shortage cause, region, or consequence alone are insufficient. Supplier-sourcing feasibility coverage may merge with testing/procurement coverage when both establish Apple as the buyer, the same component class, at least one shared supplier, and the same sourcing relationship, but it must remain separate from a shipment reduction caused by that component shortage. A title/lead stating that Apple is urgently procuring a named component to unblock production of a named Apple product is a direct hardware supply-chain action, even when the publisher frames the source as an outside reporter. By contrast, a supplier's earnings or profit report remains `weak` when its measured result is driven by non-Apple business and an Apple product appears only in timing or production background.

Apple-Broadcom chip, wireless component, custom ASIC, radio-frequency, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or networking-semiconductor supply agreements may merge across languages when they describe the same Apple/Broadcom partnership, extension, renewal, or supply action, including analysis that uses the same agreement to explain Apple in-house radio-chip timing.

Privacy/security vulnerabilities should merge across languages and sources only when they share the same Apple feature or platform, vulnerability, exposed data, concrete attack path, or policy action. A report about replacing a trusted Mac application's executable, bypassing macOS code-signing/security checks, or exploiting the same named boot-ROM flaw is direct Apple platform security news even when a source title omits the Apple name; cross-language reports of that exact mechanism belong in one event and must not be left weak merely because one source frames the attacker or researcher as the subject. Hide My Email real-address disclosure reports likewise belong in one privacy event. Reports that identify the same iCloud Private Relay real-IP or DNS disclosure belong in one event even when only some sources explain the WebKit, WebAuthn, DNS-prefetch, or WebTransport mechanism; the affected Apple service and exposed network data define the event, while the implementation detail is supporting evidence. Keep an iCloud offboarding/access-control failure involving former employees separate from an Apple security-research submission quota or AI-generated bug-report policy, even when both articles mention confidential files, vulnerabilities, AI companies, or Apple security; none of these security events should split by source language or merge with unrelated iCloud, email-client, App Store, or privacy commentary.

A current fraud, abuse, enforcement, or security incident centered on an Apple-operated account, service, store, payment system, or stored-value instrument is direct Apple platform news when the title or opening lead makes that Apple-owned system the object of the scheme or response. Keep device-only crime, tracking, or evidence stories `weak` when an iPhone, Apple Watch, AirTag, or Mac is merely stolen property, a tracking tool, or incidental evidence and no Apple-operated system is affected.

Chip-roadmap merge rules must preserve both product family and concrete action. MacBook/Mac M-series roadmap reports, iPhone A-series/RAM reports, and broad Apple silicon timeline reports may share chip terminology, but they should not merge unless they describe the same product family and action. A chip, executive, analyst, or other rare name found only in body context is supporting evidence and must not bridge explicit, non-overlapping event facets; differing facet wording may be reconciled only when both titles make the same named subject primary. Keep a foundry's base-price decision separate from a downstream device retail-price forecast unless both reports are actually about the same pricing action. Cross-language M-series coverage may merge when it describes the same generation transition or lineup action, such as M6 skipping Pro/Max and moving to M7; keep that separate from a named chip's unified-memory capacity/configuration, process node or foundry plan, and research or technology transferred from a cancelled project into chip design, even when all articles mention M7/M8, AI, memory, Mac, or the same source newsletter. Apple Pencil roadmap coverage may merge across languages when it reports the same new models and launch cycle; removable-battery regulation can remain supporting context inside that event when it explains the same models, but an independent regulation-only action must stay separate. iPhone board/logic-board leaks and iPhone battery-capacity leaks may merge across languages when they share the same model family or recognized product alias plus concrete numeric component evidence, but they should stay separate from camera, modem, launch-timing, production, price, and battery-regulation stories. Battery-law coverage may merge across languages when the affected Apple product family, jurisdiction, implementation year/rule, and removable/replaceable-battery action are the same; battery lifecycle percentages or the word `battery` alone must never merge that regulation with a capacity leak. Descriptive phrases such as "iPhone-style Dynamic Island" in a MacBook report are design comparisons, not an iPhone product-family anchor.

Project a broad Apple hardware roadmap only when the headline or opening lead establishes a genuine multi-product, multi-model, multi-generation, or structured identifier report and the page supplies independently owned product-and-action facts. Ordinary conjunctions, a secondary product mentioned only in body context, a historical comparison, or a speculative `could also be used in` sentence must not create synthetic child events. An opening lead that explicitly coordinates multiple independently supported programs may complete a narrower headline, but each child receives only facts owned by its concrete predicate; within a same-family structured section, a named model or generation heading owns its continuation facts until the next heading, while a relative clause, historical alias, or competitor comparison cannot change that ownership. Projected children from separate aggregate reports may reconcile only when the parent reports independently overlap on multiple child boundaries and the child boundary is identical; one shared product is not enough to equate the reports. Preserve concrete product, action, chip generation, model code, release period, and cancellation/retention/launch boundaries: compatible sparse translations may join an explicit action, but an aggregate projection must remain separate from a focused launch, production, price, cancellation, retention, or feature report even when one fact overlaps, because product identity is not proof of the same reporting action. Structured conflicts must prevent incompatible reports from sharing an event, while an exact product-action-qualifier identity may reconcile translated provisional product names. Reports with different explicit periods or predicates remain separate by default; when they identify the same named report owner, same product, and same concrete action, treat translated or differently bounded periods as facts within one event and preserve every stated bound rather than inventing separate actions. Likewise, a current shipment/share/performance report and a future product roadmap remain separate even when both name the same product family.

A section's product owner does not establish every sentence's speaker. An independently unattributed launch or availability prediction after a reported claim remains captured for review instead of inheriting the preceding attribution as a required fact; preserve quoted continuations, independently attributed forecasts and operating conditions.

If the brief is long, condense wording inside each event bullet. Never shorten the brief by creating theme bullets that span independent JSON events, and never drop concrete details from `title`, `summary`, or `key_facts`.

Before extracting summary and key facts, prefer a source's authoritative structured `NewsArticle.articleBody` when the visible page container includes author-follow widgets, topic-follow widgets, or popularity cards. The Verge pages should use this structured body when present.

Before extracting summary and key facts, remove source-tail affiliate recommendation blocks that follow the real article body. Common anchors include `My favorite Apple accessory recommendations`, `My favorite Apple Watch links` and equivalent `My favorite <Apple product> links`, `Worth checking out on Amazon`, `Chance's favorites`, `Official Apple Store on Amazon`, `Certified refurbished products ... off at apple.com`, `Amazon Prime Day`, `Best Apple Watch and iPhone accessories`, and `Best ... accessories/deals`. Do not remove the actual body before those anchors.

Use the earliest precise public timestamp among merged articles to decide whether the event is inside the 24-hour window. If a later article has a clearly timestamped `Update:` with substantive new information, treat that update as a new candidate event.

For deterministic exact identity, use only the detail-page title and first substantive lead sentence. Later summary text, related-story links, recommendations, and body background must not supply an exact facet that overrides incompatible title-led platform or action predicates.

A current court or jury damages verdict against Apple is direct legal news even when the patent holder is a third party. Bind the named claimant and procedural action from the affirmative source assertion; a historical recap, unissued verdict, or a different claimant must not inherit that action.

Treat a source list flattened into paragraphs with visible bullet markers as structured evidence too. A single-feature bullet must reach the fact plan or explicit review queue with its exact source span; it must not disappear merely because it lacks several comma-separated values. Existing ownership and editorial-list exclusions still apply.

## Final Brief Style

- Chinese output.
- Two sections only: `软件与系统`, `硬件与产品`.
- Each item should integrate important details into readable prose.
- Use each event's `must_include_facts` and `key_facts` before writing the brief; the distinct material facts and qualifiers in `must_include_facts` must appear in that event's final bullet, applying the [fact-versus-interpretation distinction](../SKILL.md#2-use-the-structured-result) before building the checklist; source wording or an author's added inference is not itself a coverage obligation. Material descriptive attributes and attributed explanations about the action’s design or operating limits require coverage too; numeric checks do not replace their review. `key_facts` are required source material for review, extracted from numeric paragraphs, structured short list items, tables, feature lists, official explanations, and official responses. Preserve every material fact of a retained action; a comparison or another actor’s claim keeps its own attribution and must not be rewritten as that action’s fact. Do not omit material details merely to shorten the brief. Preserve material enumerations from official sources, including fraud/safety report figures, App Store review counts, eligible countries or regions, complete per-model or per-region price/support tables, device model lists, feature names, terms, dates, rollout limits, localized features, input methods, regional rollouts, compatibility lists, feature lists, stated reasons, acknowledgements, commitments, follow-up positions, and other implementation details surfaced in `title`, `summary`, `must_include_facts`, or `key_facts`; when a source provides a coherent multi-row data series, keep every distinct row in order rather than retaining only a sample. Compress phrasing when needed, but do not collapse the list, official rationale, or response substance into a vague summary.
- Preserve every row of a source list, including short labeled numeric rows introduced by a colon rather than an explicit “includes”. Display-length thresholds must not silently remove one member; unresolved ownership stays in source review. Deliver the local context needed to interpret an unassigned list as exact evidence spans too, not only in the review reason; this does not promote that context into event facts.
- Source-local, nonnumeric physical attributes, mechanisms, operating forms, supported-device conditions, authentication prerequisites, opt-outs, attributed design or supply limits, billing caveats, and same-work synopsis details may also appear in event facts with exact source spans. A new device need not have a catalogued product name: bind its local codename to the established first-party action, and stop inheriting that ownership when the source changes subjects. Concrete retrieval examples, existing functional restrictions, and another stop or attributed result within the same reported visit belong to this material too; a detachable editorial clause must not discard the neighboring factual proposition. Keep their attribution and uncertainty; do not promote an author's opinion or a different work into the selected action.
- Apply event eligibility to the selected action, not independently to each supporting fact. Material facts about the same named broadcast or work, including participants, results and the history explaining its current schedule/location, can complete that event without changing Apple's subscription terms. Keep these facts attributed within the retained item; unrelated sporting/entertainment news still does not qualify on its own. A retained event's `must_include_facts` remains a semantic coverage requirement under that distinction; excluding an added author inference does not permit discarding its independently relevant facts or other material background. Other omissions require a demonstrated source or ownership error.
- Legal evidence review also preserves individual verdict findings and attributed party responses omitted by numeric fact selection. A quoted statement retains its adjacent attribution and time context. Assign these fragments to the matching ruling or response action with their supporting URL; their presence does not merge those actions or make a historical response current.
- Coverage comes before brevity: include every eligible event, not just a hand-picked top set. Treat the current JSON `events` as an eligible-event queue, `required_final_brief_titles` and `final_brief_queue` as the coverage checklist, and `brief_output` / `final_brief_markdown` as compact current-run scaffolds, not as loose source material for freeform recombination; preserve scaffold bullet boundaries by default. A smaller event that has a concrete Apple product, system, service/content, local-language, regional-availability, developer-platform, privacy/security, health/accessibility, or built-in-app change must still appear in the final brief when it passed inclusion. Before finalizing, create a mechanical current-JSON accounting pass: every bullet must map to one or more event ids from the just-written JSON, every eligible event id must map exactly once to a bullet, an explicit same-subject/action duplicate merge, or a named exclusion, no bullet may come from older output or memory, and every `required: true` queue item must be accounted for. Facts and source links from another eligible event ID remain outside the current bullet even when they supply causal, market, or consequence context; keep that event as its own bullet or record an explicit exclusion instead of importing only its facts and URL. Also compare the union of retained event source URLs with the draft's Markdown links and resolve every missing or extra URL before finishing. Do not drop eligible granular events just because a same-day major OS, WWDC, service, entertainment/content, or platform story is more prominent or speculative; when volume is high, shorten wording within each event's bullet instead of combining independent events into broad theme paragraphs. Keep independent Apple Wallet/Digital ID identity verification, Apple Cash split-payment, App Store policy/commission, Apple Pay/Card promotion, Apple TV+ content, Apple Music, and Apple services offer items separate unless source review proves they are duplicate coverage of the same action.
- Speculative wording such as `may`, `could`, `reportedly`, `消息称`, or `或将`, and single-source coverage from a recognized source, are not omission reasons when the crawler kept the event in `events`; write it with proper attribution instead of silently dropping it.
- Use as many sentences as the material facts require, with no target count. Apply the sentence-necessity check in [the drafting workflow](../SKILL.md#4-draft-and-check-in-small-batches) before marking a paragraph checked.
- Source links go only at the end of each item. Keep every distinct URL from the reviewed event's current-run `event.sources[]` as a Markdown link, including multiple articles from the same publisher; overlapping facts, source-list length, or another retained article from that publisher is not a reason to omit a URL. Before finalizing, compare each bullet's source-URL set with its JSON event and resolve every difference. Omit a source only when page review proves that specific URL is unrelated cluster noise, a canonical duplicate URL, or belongs to an explicitly excluded event fragment; retain and integrate every source that contributes independent facts, feature lists, compatibility or implementation details, local context, or materially different framing. Format: `（来源：[MacRumors](...), [9to5Mac](...)）`.
- Do not append diagnostics, methodology notes, source-by-source explanations, run status, network permission notes, cache paths, memory-update status, event-grouping/debug judgments, or any other operational note. When you split a mixed or suspicious cluster, write the resulting item as a clean standalone news brief; never say it was `mismerged`, `mistakenly merged`, `被误并入`, `误聚类`, or similar.


### Source-local report boundaries

- Shared products or actions found only in background paragraphs cannot establish a match. The headline action and the source-owned claim must agree; release waves retain their complete OS version, release stage and candidate number. A comparison with another release cannot supply those qualifiers. Named capabilities keep their own rollout and support conditions; neither a shared host device nor an unrelated support sentence proves the same feature or reverses a denial.
- General PC/Mac distribution compatibility and an independent app's minimum-OS change are third-party actions. They do not become Apple OS support news. Explicit Apple co-development, current observed hardware changes, and quantified new measurements retain their own evidence and qualifiers.
- Software-code disclosures own their capability, not a forecast hardware launch. Bind independently supported capabilities to their exact source spans; keep unassigned forecasts, restrictions, and additional findings in the review queue. Conflicting stated code versions prevent merging. A version's feature inventory must not be assigned wholesale to one listed feature.
- A current factual assertion can occur after historical background. Recover it from its source unit, preserving that unit's ownership and uncertainty; a speculative or negated assertion cannot supply a current claim. Historical quota changes remain context, and current auction reports retain their sale window.

A current corporate visit or institutional attendance by an Apple executive belongs to hardware/products. A personal leisure sighting or private holiday remains weak; another business activity mentioned as background does not turn it into a company action. Bind the visiting person, destination and planned/occurred/concluded stage from that action; an imminent ending is not a completed visit. A prior appointment or departure mentioned as “after” background is not its event identity, and a device demonstration does not turn the visit into a software release. Keep the visit itinerary separate from a meeting with a named counterparty and its substantive commitments or responses; a source covering both supports each only through its own passages. Another company’s visitor, an explicit denial and different destinations or stages cannot borrow that visit identity.

Resolve named Apple campaigns, brand partnership teasers, content premieres and current implementation reports by their own subject and action, including translated names explicitly supplied in the source. Preserve different partners, models, periods and announcement stages. A campaign's featured inventory and a joint filing's attributed positions can support their respective action without becoming separate releases or court conclusions. Product sections retain their own parameters and short enumerations; references to another device's interface or processor history do not create a second product event. A quoted buying opinion, podcast recap or editorial shipping outlook does not become a current Apple action because it repeats specifications or launch dates.


A sector shipment report may report Apple's observed direction without an exact count. Preserve that source-owned result, including sales/share directions with a local explanatory modifier, through fact selection and admission; require the market, reporting period and provider, and distinguish observed results from forecasts, historical comparisons and another vendor's figures. Numeric and direction-only accounts may identify the same current report; preserve their own metric roles and report qualifiers. Vendor-neutral headlines grant detail inspection, not automatic inclusion.

A writer’s inferred purpose, preferred use case or qualitative outlook remains commentary when the title and first-person assessment own the report; discussing a launch does not establish a new roadmap. Preserve a concrete release or capability report and distinguish a reported speaker’s statement from the writer’s own evaluation.

A current device introduction owns its feature inventory, compatible accessories and language prerequisites. Equivalent affirmative launch verbs and current-period wording such as this week bind the same introduction, while future, historical, negative and software-release statements keep their distinct scope. An explicitly current introduction may use its previous model as a configuration baseline: a comparison headline or table alone does not establish a second action. Preserve old/new model roles and protect independent measurements, focused support or repair reports, competitor comparisons and later changes. Those supporting conditions must not replace the launch or create roadmaps for host devices. Independently focused physical specifications, support conditions, code discoveries, repair fees, installation requirements, regional availability and later price revisions retain their own actions. A launch recap in their opening does not replace that focus. Software capabilities, including dedicated inventories of current operating functions, belong to software/systems even when their host is a hardware product, no system version appears in the headline, or the opening mentions its shipping date; an explicitly co-developed hardware collection retains its shared announcement and constituent models. A focused report on that collection's app compatibility belongs to its software operation, preserving the named app and support polarity; required hubs do not become hardware actions.

When the source opening establishes the current device introduction, its already-open ordering and scheduled delivery details remain part of that introduction. Resolve an explicitly named device behind a descriptive headline from that opening. Do not infer a later ordering stage from a separate headline; independently opening, reopening, delayed orders or delivery starts retain their own stage.

Reveal footage explicitly presented as part of that introduction supplies launch material, not a separate device roadmap. An independent film or advertising campaign remains its own publication action; a device named in its title does not make that device the object of the release. An affirmative current introduction can describe the device as long rumored without making the introduction itself a rumor.

Apple's direct guidance or training for developers adapting to its devices or platforms is a first-party software developer operation. Bind the provider and adaptation target in the same assertion; a hardware launch used as context, third-party instruction or a background Apple-device mention cannot own that operation.

A software-model registration added to device certification belongs to software/systems. Bind the recorded model identifier and certificate renewal date, rather than the headline's predicted launch or the host-device list; registration is not service availability. Reports of the same website support clue may use captured body evidence, including image alternative text, even when that evidence is absent from the selected facts. Preserve uncertainty and subsequent corrections to that clue.

Normalize current component changes and partner announcements across languages while retaining versions, generations, regions, partners and stages. Known reported platform names remain eligible for detail inspection when a short headline lacks a release verb; name recognition does not confirm a launch or override another actor’s ownership. A post-verdict motion is distinct from the original ruling and a plan to appeal; classify it by the disputed subject. If that subject is absent, retain an otherwise eligible filing and require category review; the provisional output column is not evidence of a software dispute. An author's or quoted reviewer's evaluation, hands-on impressions or old-rumor preview cannot borrow a background launch to become a new strong action; separately reported operating facts retain their own eligibility. Preserve asserted Apple invitations in source facts or the review queue without treating an undated invitation as proof of a newly issued announcement.

Bind translated component specifications to the named device's own assertion, including built-in environmental sensors. Keep hardware specifications separate from later software activation, another generation, a denial or a third-party compatible accessory. A shortened platform name may share a rollout plan only when its own opening names the canonical platform and Apple's plan; preserve the region, software version and stated month rather than treating supplier background as a separate announcement.

A production schedule binds the model in the local production assertion, its planned or actual start, and its stated production date. A sparse headline may resolve that action from its own attributed production passage; a source that omits the date retains that uncertainty. A preceding model's sales or a following retail launch date is background, not that schedule's object or date. Keep distinct models, stages and explicit schedule conflicts separate. A causal background sales clause cannot replace the primary production assertion with a market-measurement event; a primary measured result keeps its own provider and period.

Reports of the same official regional repair fee table share one software/service-price disclosure, including component-specific headlines and covered/uncovered service tiers. A website quoting an outside provider does not make that provider’s fees an Apple table. Bind region and currency to the reported table and its own price details, including an explicit continuation within the same paragraph; a later foreign-price comparison cannot change that identity. Preserve different models, regions, providers and later price changes; do not turn previously known coverage fees into a second new disclosure. A writer’s purchase-value argument remains buying advice; its specific price and operating facts may support the retained service event without qualifying the whole argument as a new hardware action. Distinguish that argument from an attributed speaker’s explanation. Affirmative compatibility phrasing may establish a current support report without a release verb; bind the supported device and operation, and do not borrow support from a related model, denial or wish.


Current source assertions also govern these focused report boundaries:

Classify the reported operation before its context: physical defects and official repair responses belong to hardware/products; developer documentation and device software capabilities belong to software/systems. A camera application is distinct from a physical home camera. Bind each event date to its own timezone, each transaction to its acquired counterparty and disclosure stage, and each physical incident to its location and stated occurrence date. Descriptive company labels, publication dates and comparison products cannot replace those anchors.

Keep each assertion's subject, action, object, polarity, reporting role and exact source span together. Binding identifies a relationship; eligibility separately determines whether it belongs in the brief. Unresolved relationships remain in evidence review, including sources that produce no event. A generated title, summary or lookup key cannot supply missing ownership or publication eligibility. Historical, hypothetical and neighboring assertions must not supply the current assertion's identity, conditions or tool version.

Explicit minimum and preferred eligibility terms remain reviewable even when they contain no numbers or device name. Preserve their original subject and timing; a review item does not assign another actor's or historical requirements to the current action.

Observation dates identify a reported dataset when explicitly stated; publication time does not replace a missing observation date. Different reported values may describe the same identified dataset and retain their source attribution. Without a shared dataset, matching values alone are insufficient. A feature inventory may inherit its subject within an owned item, but each item's explicit actor and modality take precedence; an operation's later instructions do not change the main assertion's status.

- Current first-party operating explanations, version feature inventories and newly published built-in app interfaces belong to software/systems. A platform's current device support and payment-bank enrollment are interoperability milestones; ordinary apps, old support and another version cannot borrow that relationship. Bind only the Apple-facing operation and its conditions.
- A repair-parts catalogue expansion groups its current covered models and preserves their generations; neighboring component production does not add models to that catalogue. A newly announced manufacturing partnership batch groups the named current partners and commitments. Historical models, existing suppliers and reiterated plans cannot supply that batch's identity or become new starts. Vacancy reports bind the current role; an older or separately advertised role cannot replace it, and staff assignments are not vacancies. Limit changes belong to Apple's retail action even when the lead discusses resale prices.
- A current physical observation binds the observed behavior, device and reporting context. Quantified workloads require the current tested devices, configuration and result, rather than a forecast or another model's specifications. An explicit achieved execution rate with its current configuration and method can establish a measurement without the literal word “test”; keep the result with its grammatical device subject and preserve the observer and method context when comparing reports. Earlier symptoms cited as background do not identify the new observation. Preserve demonstration-unit status, uncertainty, absence of durability testing and company responses. A self-contained dated official event announcement inside a roundup retains its media arrangements; surrounding predictions do not become an official product inventory.
- A regulator's consultation and a company's current response are separate actions. Keep the proposal, jurisdiction, explicit consultation deadline and stage together; historical statements and a withdrawn consultation cannot become current affirmative actions. A proposal's current stage may follow its locally identified regulator; another sentence's denial or a statement that the proposal is not yet law does not negate that stage. A recognized regulatory-access report with unresolved ownership or stage stays in evidence review; background hardware components cannot turn it into a device-specification report.
- Native apps, paired-device apps, widgets and explicit support exclusions belong to the host platform’s software capability. Keep each condition and correction attributed; a lack of native support is still a reported capability boundary. Tutorials, wishes and subjective evaluations cannot borrow the launch or compatibility inventory to become new events.
- A concrete market close retains its trading day and closing stage; historical intraday records are context. Benchmark reports retain the tested device, tool/version and score configuration, including locally bound CPU, GPU and AI results with intact decimal and thousands separators. Internal model identifiers and comparison devices do not create separate roadmaps. Attributed regional rollout plans retain their region, release version, timing and uncertainty; take timing from the owned plan rather than the article dateline, without requiring a particular publisher's name. Regulatory clearance and actual availability are separate stages.
- Third-party app rankings or publisher concentration in App Store charts belong to those apps or publishers; the marketplace name alone does not make them Apple actions. Apple's own chart publication, ranking-policy change or first-party app result retains its separate eligibility assessment.
- A media invitation binds the inviter, media recipient and gathering before the product inventory. A source led by another action keeps that primary action; an incidental invitation remains supporting evidence. A secondary invitation is independent only when a local attributed report, its explicit immediate reference, or a signed passage owns that proposition; attribution cannot cross an unrelated sentence. An explicit launch confirmation keeps its media logistics as supporting facts. The gathering's date is distinct from an invitation sending or response date, an earlier launch or another subject's activity. Month abbreviations and full names share the same representation. A missing or contradictory gathering date leaves the event boundary unresolved without weakening an otherwise established current Apple invitation. Unresolved ownership stays in evidence review; it cannot fall back to a hardware disclosure. An invitation discussed as the background of an opinion does not become an independent current report. Reports of the same gathering can share an event despite different headline products, without asserting that products will be announced on that day.
- Leadership interviews require a source-bound current company role and a concrete responsibility or attributed company decision. A name, profile, cover appearance or list of discussion topics does not establish that decision. Company-wide responsibilities belong to hardware/products; a decision about a specific software or service policy keeps that functional category. Role and publication evidence need not become selected news facts, but must retain valid source spans. A shared speaker and publisher alone do not establish the same interview instance; preserve an unresolved boundary when that evidence is missing.
- In trade-secret proceedings, bind the current filing party, recipient and procedural act. A new allegation, a voluntary non-use undertaking and a court order remain distinct even when each report repeats the others as background.
- An official creative profile is a current product showcase when the source establishes Apple’s publication of the named creator’s work. Resolve creator names across languages and keep separately named works distinct. Ordinary independent use of an Apple device remains weak. A current completed auction of a named historical Apple device is hardware news; accompanying letters and signatures identify the artifact rather than creating separate sales. Bind the named artifact to the sale predicate; generic product descriptions and later comparison sales cannot supply another artifact name. Current institutional attendance remains company/hardware news when the headline describes its television coverage.

Collection admission is distinct from publication eligibility: an explicit Apple-subject report does not need an RSS excerpt or a recognized model name before detail inspection. Observed market summaries may place Apple only in the body. Inspect those reports, then require Apple’s own current result; do not admit unrelated products solely to reproduce an earlier candidate count.

Current lifecycle-list assignments and ordering schedules require an owned object and local timing; a reported repair consequence is not required for a list update. Preserve counterpart roles and source wording through projection. A named media work made available for playback supplies a content object, not an Apple publication action or a hardware release. A current attributed disclosure can follow a retrospective introduction; its actor and object still need independent binding.
